# 大厂中的功劳争取与分配

### 文章背景与核心概要
许多软件工程师带着“校园幻想”进入大型科技公司，误以为只要默默做好本职工作就会自然获得优异绩效和回报。然而真实情况是，大型软件系统过于复杂，管理者无法独立评估每个人的实际贡献，职场高度依赖非正式网络与内部政治。想要成功，工程师必须主动为自己的工作争取功劳。但独占功劳往往适得其反，最成功的工程师反而懂得“大方将功劳分享出去”，以此建立同盟、扩大支持者阵营，并在发生故障时避免承受不成比例的甩锅。

---

## 📌 概要

许多软件工程师带着“校园幻想”进入大型科技公司——坚信只要默默做好工作就自然会获得优秀绩效和丰厚回报。但在现实中，大型软件系统极其复杂，管理者无法独立评估所有细节，职场很大程度上依赖于非正式网络和内部政治。要想取得成功，你必须主动为自己的工作争取功劳（Take credit）。然而，独吞功劳是适得其反的；最成功的工程师往往会**大方地把功劳分享出去**（Give away credit），以建立同盟、增加自己的支持者，并在出问题时保护自己免受不成比例的指责。

> Many software engineers enter big tech with a "school fantasy"—the belief that doing good work quietly will naturally earn them top grades and rewards. In reality, large software systems are too complex for managers to independently assess, and workplaces rely heavily on informal networks and internal politics. Succeeding requires actively taking credit for your work. However, hoarding credit is counterproductive; the most successful engineers **freely give credit away** to build alliances, multiply their advocates, and protect themselves from disproportionate blame when things go wrong.

---

## 1. “校园幻想”与真实职场

工程师们经常抱怨说，让自己的工作成果变得可见应该是经理的职责，这样他们才能专注于纯粹的代码编写。

> Engineers often complain that making their work visible should be their manager's job, allowing them to focus purely on writing code. 

这种态度源于**校园幻想**：即期望职场运作得像大学一样，只要稳妥地完成布置的作业就能拿到好成绩。大型科技公司通过将办公室称为“园区（Campuses）”来积极鼓吹这种幻觉，以吸引聪明的毕业生。然而，这依然是工作，而不是学校。如果你把它当成学术环境来对待，你注定会感到挫败。

> This attitude stems from the **school fantasy**: the expectation that the workplace operates like a university, where doing assigned work reliably earns you a good grade. Big tech companies actively encourage this illusion by calling their offices "campuses" to attract smart graduates. However, it is still work, not school. If you treat it like an academic environment, you are setting yourself up for frustration.

---

## 2. 主动争取功劳

许多新工程师面临的残忍一课是：**你必须主动为自己的工作争取功劳。**

> A harsh lesson for many new engineers is that **you must actively take credit for your work**. 

* **隐形的施援者：** 如果你在团队之外默默挽救了一个陷入困境的项目，功劳自然会流向项目负责人——更糟糕的是，你的经理可能会认为你的分心是一种缺乏专注的表现。
  > * **The Invisible Helper:** If you quietly rescue a struggling project outside your team, the credit will naturally flow to the project lead—or worse, your manager may view your straying as a lack of focus.

* **经理的盲区：** 与学校里老师打分不同，你自己才是自己工作的领域专家。软件非常复杂，经理根本没有资格单凭自己评估你的技术表现。相反，他们依赖受信任的资深工程师来评估你的产出，在有毒的文化中，这可能会导致带有偏见的反馈、主观猜测甚至职场陷害。
  > * **The Manager’s Blind Spot:** Unlike school, where a teacher grades assignments, you are the subject-matter expert on your own work. Software is so complex that managers are simply not qualified to assess your technical performance on their own. Instead, they rely on trusted senior engineers to gauge your output, which can lead to biased feedback, guesswork, or even workplace sabotage in toxic cultures.

* **自我宣传的艺术：** 经验丰富的工程师懂得如何主张功劳。他们会将取得的胜利告知经理，撰写详细介绍其解决方案的内部技术文章，并在管理链条中建立信任。参与其中只是应对企业现实的一部分。
  > * **The Art of Self-Promotion:** Experienced engineers know how to claim credit. They inform their manager of wins, write technical internal posts detailing their solutions, and build trust across management chains. Engaging in this is simply part of navigating corporate reality.

---

## 3. 大方分享功劳

一个只知道独揽功劳的工程师——通常依赖一份只关注个人成就的“夸夸文档（Brag doc）”——忽略了一个关键的动态：**往往通过将功劳分享出去，你反而能积累最多的功劳。**

> An engineer who only knows how to hoard credit—often relying on a "brag doc" focused solely on solo achievements—misses a crucial dynamic: **you can often accumulate credit best by giving it away.**

在大公司内部，升职和加薪很少由单一的直属经理说了算。相反，决定取决于一个由受信任工程师组成的隐藏网络，他们会非正式地评价你工作的价值。让这些人站在你这一边至关重要：

> Inside large companies, promotions and raises are rarely decided by a single line manager. Instead, decisions rely on a hidden network of trusted engineers who informally judge the value of your work. Getting these people on your side is vital:

* **共享聚光灯：** 在公开场合感谢合作者，在你的项目文档中提及他们，并将其他人吸纳到你的个人项目中，将其转化为团队努力成果。
  > * **Share the Spotlight:** Publicly thank collaborators, mention them in your project posts, and involve others in your individual projects to turn them into group efforts.

* **共同投入：** 一个共享的项目能为所有人带来光彩——你、你的同事以及你的经理。因为其他人也在项目的成功中占有股份，他们完全有理由去夸赞它并支持你。相反，嫉妒地死守项目会让你显得格格不入，并且让你在内部失去支持者。
  > * **Mutual Investment:** A shared project reflects well on everyone—you, your peers, and your manager. Because others have a stake in the project's success, they have every reason to talk it up and support you. Conversely, jealously guarding a project makes you look antisocial and leaves you with no internal advocates.

---

## 4. 镜像问题：归责

追责（Blame）的运作规则与功劳完全相同。当发生复杂的技术故障时，经理会咨询他们的网络，以查明是谁搞砸了。因为软件系统是深度交织在一起的，有能力的工程师通常可以把责任归咎于他们想归咎的任何地方。

> Blame operates by the exact same rules as credit. When a complex technical failure occurs, managers consult their networks to find out who screwed up. Because software systems are deeply interconnected, competent engineers can usually assign blame wherever they want.

* **孤立的风险：** 如果你是某个新项目中唯一独揽功劳的人，你就会成为避雷针。当出问题时，更广泛的工程师网络自然会有动力去指责那个他们没有参与过的、陌生且孤立的组件。
  > * **The Risk of Isolation:** If you are the sole person taking credit for a new project, you become a lightning rod. When something goes wrong, the broader network of engineers will naturally be incentivized to fault the unfamiliar, isolated component they had no part in. 

* **协作的保护力量：** 如果同一个工程师网络参与了你的项目并共享了功劳，他们维护该项目的可能性要高得多，而不太可能把所有责任单独推到你身上。
  > * **The Protective Power of Collaboration:** If that same network was involved in your project and shared the credit, they are far more likely to defend it and less inclined to shift blame solely onto you.

---

## 5. 总结

功劳与归责是大厂真正的通用货币。因为经理依赖非公开的主观对话来评估技术角色，所以埋头苦干绝不足以保驾护航。

> Credit and blame are the true currencies of big tech. Because managers rely on quiet, subjective conversations to evaluate technical roles, putting your head down and doing good work is never enough. 

你必须通过 1:1 面谈、内部文章或其他渠道沟通你的影响力。然而，自我宣传也是有限度的。**独占功劳之所以会失败，原因有二：**
1. 分享功劳能让其他人为你的成功发声力挺。
2. 将功劳完全集中在自己身上会让你陷入孤立，并在出现复杂问题时容易承担全部责任。

> You must communicate your impact through 1:1s, internal posts, or other channels. Yet, self-promotion has its limits. **Hoarding credit fails for two reasons:**
> 1. Sharing credit gets others to advocate for your success.
> 2. Concentrating credit entirely on yourself leaves you isolated and vulnerable to bearing the full weight of blame when complex problems arise.
