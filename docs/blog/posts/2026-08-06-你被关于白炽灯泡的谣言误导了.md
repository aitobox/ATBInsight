# 你被关于白炽灯泡的谣言误导了

### 文章背景与核心概要
大众普遍认为，20 世纪的灯泡太阳神卡特尔（Phoebus Cartel）出于商业贪婪秘密串通，将灯泡寿命强制限制在 1,000 小时。本文揭示了这一流行叙事的误区：虽然制造厂商确实制定了标准，但“寿命越长的灯泡就越好”的假设忽略了白炽灯的基本物理学规律。在温度、发光效率与寿命的物理权衡下，故意延长白炽灯寿命反而会在发光效率、亮度、光色以及电费成本等所有可测量维度上带来全面劣化。1,000 小时的标准实质上是为了防止消费者因盲目追求长寿命而陷入恶性竞争与劣质高耗能产品的误区。

---

## 迷思的起源

> ## The Origin of the Myth

有一个广为流传的故事，大概是这样的：

> There's a story that goes something like this:

> 1925年，多家灯泡制造商秘密串通，将灯泡寿命统一标准化为 1,000 小时，并互相测试对方的产品以确保合规。
> 
> 在当时，许多灯泡的实际寿命都远超一千小时。
> 
> 因此，他们这么做是为了让人们不得不源源不断地购买更多灯泡。

> > In 1925, lightbulb manufacturers secretly colluded to standardize lifespans at 1,000 hours, testing each other's products to ensure compliance.
> > 
> > At the time, many bulbs lasted longer than one thousand hours.
> > 
> > Therefore, this was done so that people would always need to buy more lightbulbs.

**这是错误的。** 但这种错误巧妙地引用了真实事实，并隐匿在人们绝不会去查证的前提假设之中：*即“寿命更长的灯泡就是好产品”这一假设。*

> **This is wrong.** But it's the type of wrong that cites its sources and hides in the part you'd never think to fact-check: *the assumption that a longer-lasting lightbulb is a good product.* 

事实上，增加白炽灯泡的寿命会在其他所有维度上使其性能恶化。制定标准的初衷，主要是为了避免由消费者心理驱动的“劣币驱逐良币”的恶性竞争。

> In truth, increasing the lifespan of an incandescent bulb makes it worse in every other way. The purpose of standardizing was primarily to avoid a race to the bottom driven by consumer psychology.

## 白炽发光的物理学原理

> ## The Physics of Incandescence

传统白炽灯泡是一个相当简单的设备：一根密封在玻璃泡内的细钨丝（约 20 μm），以防止其暴露于空气中。通电时，钨丝被加热至白炽状态并开始发光。

> An old-school lightbulb is a rather simple device: a thin tungsten wire (~20 μm) sealed inside a glass envelope to protect it from air. When current is applied, the wire gets white-hot and starts glowing.

灯泡最重要的参数是灯丝达到的温度。这决定了峰值发射波长（光色）和灯泡的亮度。

> The most important parameter of a lightbulb is how hot that wire gets. This controls the peak emission wavelength (color) and brightness of the lamp.

* 室温下的物体也会发射电磁波（这就是热成像仪的工作原理），但波长在 ~10 μm 范围，而非我们肉眼可见的 400 nm–700 nm 可见光。
* 要将发射峰值推入可见光光谱，灯丝温度需要达到 ~5,700 °C——即**太阳表面的温度**。
* 没有任何金属能够承受如此高温；钨的熔点“只有”3,422 °C。

> * Room-temperature objects emit light (this is how thermal cameras work), but it's at the ~10 μm range instead of the 400 nm–700 nm light that we can see.
> * To put the emission peak in the visible spectrum, the filament would need to run at ~5,700 °C—the temperature of **the sun**.
> * No metal can survive these conditions; tungsten melts at "only" 3,422 °C.

### 灯丝材料的简要历史

> ### A Brief History of Filament Materials

* **钨（Tungsten）：** 拥有所有金属中最高的熔点，自然成为灯丝的首选。然而，金属钨相当脆弱，拉制成细丝并不容易。
* **碳（Carbon）：** 早期的商业化灯泡使用通过将植物纤维炭化制成的碳灯丝。但是，碳在相当温和的温度下（~2,000 °C）就会挥发。
* **钽（Tantalum）：** 20 世纪 10 年代曾短暂应用，因为这种金属比钨更容易拉成细丝。这是第一批真正可以整夜通电使用的灯泡，尽管随着钨加工技术的进步很快被淘汰。
* **二氧化锆（Zirconium Dioxide）：** 某些实验使用了加热后具有导电性的陶瓷材料。这使得灯泡可以在空气中运行（省去了真空泵和玻璃密封的需求），但受限于 2,700 °C 的熔点。

> * **Tungsten:** Having the highest melting point of any metal, tungsten is the obvious choice for filaments. However, the metal is quite brittle, and drawing it into a wire isn't easy.
> * **Carbon:** The first commercialized lamps used carbon filaments made by charring plant fibers. However, carbon evaporates at fairly modest temperatures (~2,000 °C).
> * **Tantalum:** Briefly produced during the 1900s because the metal was easier to draw into a wire than tungsten. These were the first bulbs that could actually be left on at night, though they were quickly replaced as tungsten manufacturing improved.
> * **Zirconium Dioxide:** Some experiments used ceramics that become conductive when heated. These allowed lamps to operate in air (obviating the need for a vacuum pump and glass seals), but were limited by a melting point of 2,700 °C.

## 根本权衡：发光效率 vs. 寿命

> ## The Fundamental Trade-Off: Efficiency vs. Lifespan

由于任何灯丝都必须在其熔点以下运行，其峰值发射波长始终位于红外线区域。只有光谱极高端的高能边缘才能用于照明，这意味着温度的微小提升就会使灯泡的发光效率提高数个数量级，并产生更白、更明亮的光芒。

> Since any filament must run below its melting point, the peak emission is always in the infrared. Only the extreme high-energy edge of the spectrum is useful for illumination, meaning a small increase in temperature makes a lamp orders of magnitude more efficient and produces a whiter, less depressing glow.

障碍何在？当金属接近其熔点时，其原子结构几乎难以维持：
1. 炽热的钨丝会随着金属晶体彼此滑动而缓慢崩解。
2. 原子从表面不断蒸发，直到灯丝断裂。

> The snag? When a metal is close to its melting point, its atoms are barely holding together:
> 1. A hot tungsten filament slowly falls apart as metal crystals slide past each other.
> 2. Atoms evaporate from the surface until there's no wire left.

这两个过程的发生速率都会随温度急剧上升，从而在光色/发光效率与使用寿命之间构成了不可调和的根本权衡。

> The rate of both processes increases sharply with temperature, creating a fundamental trade-off between color/efficiency and lifespan.

### 百年灯泡案例研究

> ### The Centennial Light Case Study

所有人谈到“计划报废”故事时总会引用的那个百年灯泡，悬挂在加州的一个消防局里。它几乎连续运行了 120 多年，累计运行时间超过一百万小时。

> The lightbulb everyone always cites in the "planned obsolescence" story hangs in a California fire department. It has been running nearly continuously for over 120 years, racking up more than a million hours of operation. 

令人印象深刻，对吧？但几乎没人提及的是：**它实际几乎根本不发光。**

> Impressive, right? What almost no one talks about is that **it's hardly even glowing**. 

尽管名义上是一盏 60W 的灯，它实际仅消耗 4 瓦功率——并且由于发光效率极其低下，其亮度远低于正常的 4W 灯泡。很大程度上，这个灯泡当年在手工制造时出现了瑕疵，导致灯丝电阻过高。这就是为什么它被当作小夜灯出售；在其他任何用途上它都毫无实用价值。

> Despite nominally being a 60W lamp, it draws only 4 watts—and is a lot dimmer than you'd expect from a 4W lamp due to its abysmal efficiency. In all likelihood, the bulb was handmade incorrectly, resulting in a very high filament resistance. That's why it was sold as a nightlight; it wasn't usable for anything else.

早期的手工灯泡普遍针对长寿命进行了过分优化，导致发出的光线昏暗偏黄，效率仅为现代白炽灯的一小部分。一旦实现了自动化生产，新灯泡的价格便降至几美分。由于电费成本远远超过了灯泡本身的购买价格（一个 60W 的灯泡在 1,000 小时内大约消耗 6 美元电费，而灯泡售价仅 3 美元），因此安装一个寿命极长但效率低下的灯泡在经济上完全没有任何道理。

> Early handmade bulbs were universally optimized for long lives, resulting in dim, yellow light and a fraction of the efficiency of modern incandescent lamps. Once production was automated, new bulbs cost pennies. Because electricity costs vastly outweighed the cost of the bulb itself (a 60W bulb consumes roughly $6 in electricity over 1,000 hours compared to a $3 bulb price tag), installing a longer-lasting, highly inefficient bulb makes zero economic sense.

## 为何卡特尔要强制推行该标准

> ## Why the Cartel Enforced the Standard

现代白炽灯泡的寿命依然在 500 到 2,500 小时之间——刚好围绕着卡特尔制定的 1,000 小时标准。制造商并没有花费巨资去“延长白炽灯寿命”，而是将大量精力和资金投入到全新技术的研发中：如荧光灯和 LED 灯。

> Modern incandescent bulbs still last between 500 to 2,500 hours—a range right around the cartel's 1,000-hour standard. Instead of "making bulbs last longer," manufacturers spent huge amounts of time and money developing entirely new technology: fluorescent and LED lamps.

如果长寿命灯泡在客观上是更差的产品，为什么还需要卡特尔来强制执行千小时限制？

> If long-lived lightbulbs are objectively worse products, why did they need a cartel to enforce a thousand-hour limit? 

答案在于**消费者心理学**。寿命非常简单易懂。如果消费者看到两个 60W 灯泡——一个标称 400 小时，另一个标称 2,000 小时——他们会条件反射式地购买寿命较长的那一个，而完全不会考虑发光效率或电费开销。此外，历史上灯泡包装习惯用输入功率（瓦数）来代表亮度，这让比较变得更加混乱。市场竞争迫使制造厂商不得不制造性能更差、效率更低的产品，仅仅是为了在单一指标上赢得买家。

> **Consumer psychology.** Lifespan is easy to understand. If a consumer saw two 60W bulbs—one claiming 400 hours and the other 2,000 hours—they would reflexively buy the longer-lasting one without considering efficiency or electricity costs. Furthermore, bulb packaging historically used input wattage as a proxy for brightness, making comparisons confusing. Competition was forcing makers to produce worse, less efficient products just to win on a single metric.

明确地说：太阳神卡特尔（Phoebus cartel）确实参与了价格串通和反消费者行为，利润无疑是其制定标准的重要考量因素。但就本质而言，钨丝灯本身就是消耗品。

> To be clear: the Phoebus cartel engaged in price-fixing and anti-consumer practices, and profit was undoubtedly a factor in their standard. But by nature, tungsten lamps are consumable items. 

现实很少能完美契合简单的道德叙事。每一个工业制造品都是数百种妥协折中的产物，其中大多数妥协对最终用户而言是不可见的。计划报废在其他领域确实存在——但在白炽灯泡这件事上，它绝对不是一个恰当的例证。

> Reality rarely fits into neat narratives. Every manufactured object is the result of hundreds of overlapping compromises, most of which are invisible to the end user. Planned obsolescence certainly exists elsewhere—but lightbulbs just aren't a good example.

## 附言：关于 LED “迪拜灯”

> ## P.S. About LED "Dubai Lamps"

关于现代照明的一个常见反驳是“迪拜灯（Dubai Lamps）”——这是一种 LED 灯丝灯泡，其配备的发光二极管数量是标准灯泡的四倍。由于负载被分散，每个二极管仅在其额定功率的四分之一状态下运行，发热量大大降低。

> A common counterpoint brought up regarding modern lighting is "Dubai Lamps"—LED filament-style bulbs featuring four times as many diodes as standard bulbs. Because the load is distributed, each diode runs at a quarter of its rated power and stays much cooler.

* **购买渠道：** 这些并非迪拜独享；您可以在全球范围内购买到品牌为“UltraEfficient”的同类产品。
* **发光效率：** 它们实现了惊人的 ~200 lm/W 发光效率（而普通 LED 约为 ~100 lm/W），代价是制造成本更高。
* **寿命现实：** 大多数 LED 灯泡失效并不是因为二极管过热，而是由于电源模块中输入滤波电容受热损坏。尽管“迪拜灯”通过降低整体功耗减轻了部分散热压力，但与配有外部散热器的专用 LED 灯具相比，传统灯泡外形在散发热量方面天生就存在劣势。

> * **Availability:** These are not exclusive to Dubai; you can buy them globally under the branding of "UltraEfficient" bulbs.
> * **Efficiency:** They achieve an impressive ~200 lm/W instead of the usual ~100 lm/W, at the cost of being more expensive to manufacture.
> * **Lifespan Realities:** Most LED bulbs don't fail because the diodes overheat; they fail due to heat-related damage to the power supply's input-smoothing capacitor. While "Dubai lamps" mitigate some thermal issues by burning less power overall, the lightbulb form factor remains inherently poor for heat dissipation compared to dedicated LED fixtures with external heat sinking.

归根结底，“迪拜灯”依然是一种妥协产物，试图在发光效率、制造成本以及适配传统螺口灯座的限制之间寻找平衡。

> Ultimately, "Dubai lamps" are still an imperfect design caught between efficiency, manufacturing costs, and the constraints of fitting into standard legacy light sockets.
