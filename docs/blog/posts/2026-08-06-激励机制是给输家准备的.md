# 激励机制是给输家准备的

> *"只有傻瓜和懦夫才会‘对激励做出反应’。健康的人只要在合理可行的范围内就会做自己想做的事，伟大的人则无论如何都会做自己想做的事。"*  
> — Alice Maz

> *"Only morons and cowards 'respond to incentives.' Healthy people do what they want to do if reasonably practical, great people do what they want to do no matter what."*  
> — Alice Maz

---

## 📌 概要

在这篇短文中，作者批判了我们将“不良激励”视为社会和个人失败根源的现代偏执。在承认扭曲的奖励结构无处不在的同时，作者认为归咎于激励机制是一种逃避——它暗示人类仅仅是被设定好去服从任意奖励系统的棋子。真正的正直、目标感和道德勇气要求我们超越系统本身，拒绝虚无的奖励追求，并培养我们内心的精神骨气。

> In this essay, the author tackles our modern obsession with "bad incentives" as the root cause of societal and personal failures. While acknowledging that perverse reward structures exist everywhere, the author argues that blaming incentives is a cop-out—it implies that human beings are merely pawns programmed to obey arbitrary reward systems. True integrity, purpose, and moral courage require us to look past the system, reject hollow reward-seeking, and cultivate our own internal backbone.

## 无论风浪多大，带上你自己的头盔

大家想必都会同意：激励机制很糟糕。无论你看向何处，人们都在因做错事而获得回报。这已经成了解决所有问题的标准诊断，从气候变化到政治极化再到学术不端——*“抱歉了伙计，你这是得了严重的不良激励病。”*

> We can all agree: the incentives are bad. Everywhere you look, people are being rewarded for doing the wrong thing. This has become the go-to diagnosis for every problem, from climate change to political polarization to scientific malfeasance—*"sorry pal, you’ve got a bad case of the incentives."*

我并不完全反对这种分析。谁能反对呢？这基本上是个同义反复：*人们做坏事是因为他们做坏事得到了鼓励和奖励。* 额，不然你为什么要做坏事呢？

> I don’t disagree with this analysis, exactly. Who could? It’s basically tautological: *People do bad things because they are encouraged and rewarded for doing bad things.* Well, why else would you do them?

我对这种思维方式真正的抱怨在于，它暗示了激励机制*应该*被服从，服从它是正常的甚至是高尚的，而且除了服从之外别无选择。你又怎么能被期望为了——为了什么？为了做正确的事——而去打破规则、放弃晋升或牺牲哪怕零点几分的 GPA 呢？

> My real gripe with this line of thinking is that it implies incentives *should* be obeyed, that it is normal or even noble to do so, and that it is perhaps impossible to do otherwise. How could you be expected to break a rule, forego a promotion, or give up a decimal of your grade point average for the sake of—what? Doing the right thing?

这里有一个原本不言而喻的道理，但现在却无人提及：**激励机制是给输家准备的。** 仅仅因为某个任意的奖励系统会给你一点甜头就去做某事——那是信鸽该干的事，不是人类该干的事。如果你围绕满足激励机制来构建生活，那你就是一个蠢货和傻瓜。

> There’s a point here that should go without saying, but instead it has simply gone unsaid: **incentives are for losers.** Doing something simply because an arbitrary reward system will give you a lil treat—that’s for pigeons, not people. If you build your life around satisfying incentives, then you’re a dummy and a schmuck.

当我在少棒联赛打了几年灾难性的棒球时，我最不喜欢的部分就是上场击球，因为你必须戴上当地体育部门提供的那些破旧的击球头盔。没有一个是合身适尺寸的，而且闻起来就像有上百个汗流浃背的 10 岁小孩在你之前戴过一样。每一次击球不仅冒着被三振出局的风险，还冒着染上头虱的风险。

> When I played Little League baseball for a few disastrous years, my least favorite part was going up to bat because you had to wear one of those ratty old batting helmets supplied by the local rec department. None of them fit right, and they all smelled like a hundred sweaty 10-year-olds had worn them before you. Every at-bat carried not only the risk of striking out, but also the risk of contracting head lice.

有几个孩子，那些认真的孩子，带了自己的头盔——量身定制，品味高雅地贴着贴纸，大概里面零头虱。这些孩子跑垒时就像小王子一样。

> A few kids, the serious ones, brought their own helmets—custom fitted, tastefully decorated with decals, and presumably containing zero lice. These kids ran the bases like little princes.

我之所以说这些，是因为激励机制就像击球头盔：**如果你不带自己的头盔，你就只能使用别人为你提供的破头盔。**

> I say all this because incentives are like batting helmets: **if you don’t bring your own, you’ll have to use the crappy ones that are provided for you.**

人类成长中至关重要的一部分——事实上，也是人类成长中最具“人性”的一部分——就是获得一个人生目标。如果你的道德信念本该存在的地方留有空白，那么你人格中的这个空腔就会被对金钱、权力、名声和地位的肿瘤般欲望所填满。这就是那种“对激励做出反应”的人。

> A critical part of human development—in fact, the most *human* part of human development—is to acquire a purpose. If you have a void where your moral convictions should be, that cavity of your personality gets filled up with tumorous desires for money, power, fame, and status. This is the kind of person who "responds to incentives."

## 救救我，上天的救星，你是我唯一的希望

在没有经过任何正规指导的情况下，大约 100% 的人类都能学会说话，而大约 0% 的人类能自然学会阅读。说话轻松且早期就会；阅读出现得很晚且十分吃力，它依托于大脑中原本并非用于解码纸上符号的区域。

> Without any formal instruction, approximately 100% of humans will learn to speak, while approximately 0% of humans will learn to read naturally. Talking comes easy and early; reading comes late and laboriously, piggybacking on bits of the brain that were never meant to decode squiggles on paper.

在思考任何人类能力时，问一个问题都很有用：*这更像说话，还是更像阅读？*

> When thinking about any human ability, it’s useful to ask: *Is this more like speaking, or is it more like reading?*

建立价值体系似乎更类似于阅读。在 21 世纪初，社会学家 Christian Smith 和 Melinda Lundquist Denton 调查了美国青少年的宗教和道德信仰。他们得到的回应是一堆被他们称为**“道德主义治疗型一神论”（Moralistic Therapeutic Deism）**的废话。大多数青少年认为神基本上是一个飘在天空中的酷家伙，希望每个人都开心，充当着天上的管家，不需要太多其他东西。

> Developing a value system seems to be a lot more like reading. In the early 2000s, sociologists Christian Smith and Melinda Lundquist Denton asked American teenagers about their religious and moral beliefs. What they got in return was a bunch of gobbledygook they termed **"moralistic therapeutic deism."** Most teens believed God was essentially a cool dude floating in the sky who wants everyone to be happy, acts like a celestial butler, and doesn't demand much else.

我们的价值体系脆弱，原因与我们的科学推理能力脆弱是一样的：**在我们被告知认知模型崩溃之前，我们不会去修正它们。** 除非你定期被投掷到伦理风暴中，否则没有什么能促使你巩固道德信念。因此，我们对对与错的认知很容易被任何还算过得去的奖惩机制所覆写，仅仅是因为我们心智的硬盘上原本就没有写下多少东西。

> Our value systems are flimsy for the same reason our scientific reasoning is flimsy: **we don’t fix our mental models until something tells us that they’re broken.** Unless you are regularly thrust into ethical firestorms, there's nothing telling you to firm up your moral convictions. Consequently, our sense of right and wrong is easily overwritten by any half-decent system of rewards and punishments simply because we don't have much written there in the first place.

## 你必须凝视这条怪鱼并走向狂乱

你可以把 H.P. 洛夫克拉夫特（H.P. Lovecraft）的大多数短篇小说总结为：*“这家伙看到了某个极其混乱可怖的东西，以至于他彻底疯掉了。”*

> You can summarize most of H.P. Lovecraft’s short stories as: *"Guy looks at something so messed up that he goes totally bonkers."*

这是一个非常有用的比喻，因为我认为在每个人心灵深处都有某种版本的**“大怪鱼”（Big Horrible Fish）**：某种未经审视的假设，如果你完全去审视它，就会要求你推翻自己生活的很大一部分。

> This is a useful metaphor, because I think at the center of everyone’s psyche is some version of the **Big Horrible Fish**: some unexamined assumption that, if you fully considered it, would require you to upend a significant portion of your life.

我于 2021 年在旧金山参加一场学术会议时看到了我的那条“怪鱼”。在酒店内部，有数十场关于社会正义的演讲；而在酒店*外部*的 Tenderloin 街区，人们瘫倒在人行道上，死于药物过量。学者们急匆匆地从他们身边穿过前去发表论文——证实了心理学最著名研究之一所揭示的黑暗真相。

> I saw my fish in 2021 in San Francisco, during an academic conference. Inside the hotel were dozens of talks on social justice; *outside* the hotel, in the Tenderloin district, people were sprawled on the sidewalk, dying of overdoses. Scholars scurried past them to present papers—proving a dark truth from one of psychology's most famous studies.

那天我心里的某些东西解扣了。我意识到我们并不是在寻求取得实际进展；我们只是为了自己的 CV 在发表论文而已。

> Something un-clicked for me that day. I realized we weren't trying to make actual progress; we were just publishing papers for our CVs.

当我遇到拥有罕见正直品质的人——那些愿意不惜一切代价违抗激励机制的人时，我发现他们几乎总是目睹过那条“怪鱼”。他们经历了一场摧毁幻想的道德洗礼，并获得了锻造自身价值体系的机会。他们与输家截然相反。

> When I meet someone of uncommon integrity—someone willing to defy the incentives at any cost—I find they almost always have seen the fish. They have undergone a moral reckoning that shattered their illusions and gave them the chance to forge their own values. They are the opposite of a loser.

## 萨姆纳的 500 天

我过去每天都会在我大学附近看到一个那样的人的雕像：**参议员查尔斯·萨姆纳（Senator Charles Sumner）**。

> I used to see a man like that every day in statue form near my university: **Senator Charles Sumner**.

萨姆纳在波士顿的贫困环境中长大，生活在城市的黑人居民中间，并在 1830 年代前往法国旅行，在索邦大学看到黑人和白人学生作为平等的一员共同学习，这令他大吃一惊。他在日记中写道：

> Sumner grew up poor in Boston, lived among the city’s Black population, and traveled to France in the 1830s, where he was astounded to see Black and white students studying together as equals at the Sorbonne. He wrote in his diary:

> *"那么，我们之间自由黑人与白人之间的差距必然源于教育，而非存在于事物的本性之中。"*

> *"It must be then that the distance between free blacks and whites among us is derived from education, and does not exist in the nature of things."*

他成为了他那个时代最直言不讳的白人废奴主义者。1856 年，在发表了一篇具有煽动性的反奴隶制演讲后，一位名叫普雷斯顿·布鲁克斯（Preston Brooks）的支持奴隶制的国会议员在参议院桌前走到萨姆纳面前，用手杖将他打得几近致死。

> He became the most outspoken white abolitionist of his generation. In 1856, after giving an inflammatory anti-slavery speech, a pro-slavery congressman named Preston Brooks approached Sumner at his desk in the Senate and beat him nearly to death with a cane.

这是否吓退了萨姆纳？当他康复后，他变得*更加激进*，推动林肯废除奴隶制，并起草了 100 年后重新出现在《1964 年民权法案》中的修正案。萨姆纳很早就看到了那条“怪鱼”，并且他拒绝移开视线。

> Did this deter Sumner? When he recovered, he became *even more provocative*, pushing Lincoln to abolish slavery and drafting amendments that would reappear 100 years later in the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Sumner saw the fish early on, and he refused to look away.

## 停下脚爪，深思熟虑

我理解为什么人们如此担心激励机制。看着恶人功成名就令人气愤。但古德哈特定律（Goodhart's Law）和坎贝尔定律（Campbell's Law）提醒我们，每一种激励机制注定最终都会被腐化。执念于激励机制感觉就像向“猴爪”许愿——它总是以讽刺的方式适得其反。

> I understand why people worry so much about incentives. It’s infuriating to watch villains succeed. But Goodhart’s Law and Campbell’s Law remind us that every incentive is destined to be corrupted eventually. Obsessing over incentives feels like wishing on a Monkey's Paw—it always backfires ironically.

在试图修复激励机制的人与利用激励机制的人之间永恒的猫鼠游戏中，我支持猫。我们应该尝试完善我们的激励体系，但同时——**别管什么激励机制，做正确的事。**

> In the eternal game of cat and mouse between those trying to fix incentives and those exploiting them, I am rooting for the cats. We should try to get our incentive systems right, but also—**incentives be damned, do the right thing.**

如果我们寻求终结我们的永久性难题，我们就必须在自己身上培养查尔斯·萨姆纳在领袖身上最看重的三样东西：

> **“第一是骨气（Backbone），第二是骨气，第三还是骨气。”**

> > **"The first is backbone, the second is backbone, and the third is backbone."**
