# Ten Advances in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science

## Summary
Following Anthropic's recent cryptographic breakthroughs using Claude, OpenAI has showcased its next-generation internal model ("Astra") by solving ten stagnant mathematical and theoretical computer science problems. Costing under $2,000 per problem, the results include Lean 4 formalizations, a detailed paper, and reasoning walkthroughs. While these milestones are sparking philosophical debates and a "Deep Blue" style existential crisis among some mathematicians, visionaries like Terence Tao view AI not as a replacement, but as the catalyst for a collaborative new era of "big mathematics."

---

## The Latest AI Flex in Advanced Research

Just days after Anthropic made waves by [discovering cryptographic weaknesses with Claude](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/28/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses-with-claude/) (using Mythos Preview with a $100,000 token budget), OpenAI has entered the fray. 

OpenAI deployed an internal version of **Astra**—its upcoming major model—to tackle ten long-standing mathematical problems that had seen no progress on their main results for at least a decade. According to the company, each solution was achieved at a cost of less than $2,000 (calculated at GPT-5.6 Sol token prices). 

*(Note: There is currently no disclosure regarding how many failed problems were attempted without reaching a solution within the $2,000 budget.)*

### Transparency and Documentation

OpenAI has provided a commendable level of documentation alongside their announcement:
* **[openai/ten-proofs GitHub Repository](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs):** Contains Lean 4 formalizations of the results.
* **[Official Research Paper](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/ten-proofs-oai.pdf):** Describes the solutions in detail.
* **[LLM-Generated Walkthroughs](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/reasoning-walkthroughs.pdf):** A PDF where the model reconstructs how the proof came together based on unpublished reasoning traces.

Despite this transparency, many researchers are eager for one missing piece: the exact prompts used to guide the models.

---

## The Mathematical Community's "Deep Blue" Moment

Milestones like this are triggering a collective psychological shift among mathematicians, reminiscent of IBM's Deep Blue defeating Garry Kasparov. Last week, mathematician Kirwin Hampshire published an impassioned essay titled [_The Dark Night of Mathematics_](https://kirwinhampshire.substack.com/p/the-dark-night-of-mathematics), detailing a "profound spiritual crisis" brought on by earlier, less significant AI capabilities.

However, not all prominent mathematicians share this dread. Terence Tao offers a more optimistic perspective, describing a shift toward **"big mathematics"** in a [recent IEEE Spectrum interview](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-in-mathematics):

> Unlike some of his peers, Tao is neither dismissive of AI nor fearful. Instead, he sees it as the catalyst for a fundamental shift in the discipline—a transition toward what he calls “big mathematics.” He envisions a future of large-scale, decentralized collaborations between humans and machines, where complex mathematical tasks can be diced and sliced, with humans claiming the creative parts and AI doing the lion’s share of the technical grunt work.
