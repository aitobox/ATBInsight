# Datasette Code-Frequency Chart on GitHub

## Summary
In an exploration of how modern coding agents and advanced language models impact developer output, Simon Willison examines the GitHub code-frequency chart for his open-source project, **Datasette**. The data reveals a dramatic surge in code additions coinciding with the adoption of next-generation AI models.

---

## Measuring AI Impact on Developer Output

Out of curiosity, I decided to see if I could find a useful illustration of the impact of coding agents and advanced class models (such as Opus 4.5) on my own output. The best visualization I’ve found so far is the GitHub [code-frequency chart for Datasette](https://github.com/simonw/datasette/graphs/code-frequency).

![Screenshot of a GitHub "Code frequency" bar chart, subtitled "Additions and deletions per week", showing green addition bars and red deletion bars per week from 2018 through 2026, with a y-axis labeled Frequency ranging from -20k to 30k. Activity comes in sporadic bursts: the largest spike is 37,022 additions with -9,528 deletions in 2026, followed by 14,638 additions with -6,584 deletions in late 2025, 15,998 additions in early 2018, and a standout deletion spike of -10,658 in mid-2020, with quieter periods of smaller weekly changes in between.](./images/40dd0fa21957.png)

The massive spike in activity toward the end of the timeline directly aligns with the introduction and adoption of advanced AI tools, including models like **Opus 4.8**, **GPT-5.5**, **Fable 5**, and **GPT-5.6 Sol**.
