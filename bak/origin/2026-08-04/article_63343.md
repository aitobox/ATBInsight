# Circular Financing: The End of the AI Bubble?

### Summary
The AI industry’s reliance on "circular financing"—where companies invest in each other to inflate valuations and fuel growth—is showing signs of collapse. While initial deals like the Oracle-OpenAI partnership triggered massive market rallies, recent attempts by firms like Nvidia to backstop similar projects are being met with investor skepticism and falling stock prices. As the market shifts from blind optimism to scrutiny, the sustainability of the GenAI boom is increasingly in question.

---

### The Illusion of Growth
Nine months ago, the tech world was electrified by a **$300 billion deal between Oracle and OpenAI**. The market reaction was euphoric: Oracle’s stock surged by 43%, and Larry Ellison’s net worth ballooned by $100 billion. 

However, the foundation of this growth was shaky. As I noted in my essay, *[Peak Bubble](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/peak-bubble)*, the deal lacked fundamental economic logic. Oracle’s stock, which peaked at $328 following the announcement, has since plummeted to around $120, reflecting a growing market wariness toward circular financing schemes.

### Nvidia and the "Desperation" Narrative
The market’s mood has shifted dramatically. Recent reports suggest that **Nvidia is considering a $250 billion backstop** for an OpenAI-led data center project. Unlike the enthusiasm seen last September, this news triggered a sell-off, with Nvidia’s stock dropping over 4.5% in early trading. 

Investors are increasingly viewing these maneuvers not as strategic masterstrokes, but as acts of desperation. As one investor aptly described it: *"Two drunks leaning on each other to stand up."*

### A Changing Landscape
The broader market is beginning to re-evaluate the AI sector:
*   **Apple’s Ascent:** Once mocked for its perceived lack of AI investment, Apple has now overtaken Nvidia in market standing.
*   **SpaceX’s Decline:** After attempting to position itself as an AI player, the company has seen its stock drop over 25% in the last month—down more than 50% from its June highs.
*   **Creditor Caution:** Financial institutions are becoming increasingly leery of the "creative financing" and off-balance-sheet arrangements that have propped up the AI sector.

### The Verdict
The GenAI boom has been fueled by hope and circular capital rather than genuine, sustainable profits. As the era of "creative financing" faces increased scrutiny, the industry may finally be forced to confront the reality that hope is not a business model.
