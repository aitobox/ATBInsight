# Premium: AI Is Getting Way Too Expensive

## 📋 Summary
Public discussions surrounding Artificial Intelligence are disproportionately fixated on theoretical job displacement and speculative productivity gains. Major players like Anthropic and OpenAI actively promote these narratives to distract from the staggering financial reality of the industry. In truth, AI's industry-wide revenues are dwarfed by massive capital injections and staggering funding commitments. As infrastructure costs escalate, the basic economic puzzle becomes increasingly difficult to solve, forcing hyperscalers into a corner where compute costs skyrocket while viable, paying customers remain scarce.

---

## The Illusion of AI's Economic Impact

Much of the discourse regarding the benefits and risks of AI boils down to theoretical discussions: the jobs lost (or not lost) due to Large Language Models (LLMs), and the speculative productivity boosts expected to replace or augment human labor. 

Operations like [Anthropic’s Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-index?ref=wheresyoured.at) and [OpenAI’s Economic Research Exchange](https://openai.com/index/economic-research-exchange/?ref=wheresyoured.at) function primarily as marketing arms. They exist to propagate the unsubstantiated belief that LLMs are driving, or will soon drive, massive macroeconomic and productivity shifts. 

In reality, little to no concrete evidence supports this narrative. Instead, we see:
* Occasional studies showing that [LLMs can actually make workers slower and worse at their jobs](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/07/11/ai-coding-tools-make-developers-slower-study-finds/1143832?ref=wheresyoured.at).
* Cherry-picked single lines from research incorrectly used to argue that [AI is making it harder for young people to find jobs](https://www.wheresyoured.at/how-to-argue-with-an-ai-booster/#:~:text=QUIP%3A%20%22AI%20is%20so%20profoundly%20powerful%20that%20it%E2%80%99s%20causing%20young%20people%20to%20have%20trouble%20finding%20a%20job!%E2%80%9D). *(Even Anthropic’s Head of Economics [admitted recently](https://x.com/PeterMcCrory/status/2079979321607745905?ref=wheresyoured.at) that there has been "no material increase in the unemployment rate to date.")*

These conversations deliberately detract from the tangible harms and actual effects of AI. They exist to stoke fear regarding job security while ignoring the true underlying financial reality: a speculative bet on whether LLMs—a definitively niche technology—can evolve into general-purpose software ([per Roger MacNamee](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/07/06/ai-investing-structure-is-creating-a-humongous-bubble-says-elevation-partners-roger-mcnamee.html?ref=wheresyoured.at)) capable of scaling into the next Google Search, iPhone, or Microsoft 365.

---

## The Massive Revenue Disconnect

Outside of Anthropic and OpenAI, the AI industry’s revenues remain remarkably small. 

According to [Exponential View’s deliberately pro-industry analysis](https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-ai-industry-is-losing/#exponential-view-should-be-ashamed-of-itself), trailing twelve-month revenues across the *entire* industry—**including** the cloud spend of OpenAI and Anthropic—sit at roughly **$110 billion**.

To put that figure into perspective:
* It is **$12 billion less** than the [$122 billion OpenAI raised in March](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/?ref=wheresyoured.at).
* It is a staggering **$145 billion less** than what all AI startups raised combined [in just the first quarter of 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/q1-2026-ai-funding-blows-175036425.html?ref=wheresyoured.at).

By leaning heavily into theoretical labor debates, Anthropic and OpenAI draw attention away from the tangible numbers: hundreds of billions of dollars in financial commitments, the strain those commitments place on hyperscalers and chip manufacturers, and the sheer over-extension of venture capital—which [largely enables massive on-paper gains with little hope of actual liquidity](https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-enshittifinancial-crisis/#the-devil%E2%80%99s-deal-of-investing-in-ai-startups).

Ultimately, the obsession over AI’s theoretical relationship to jobs is a distraction—a conscious effort to avoid confronting the unsustainable scale of capital deployed on the flimsily-founded promises of AI labs and hyperscalers.

## What Lies Ahead

To understand whether the AI bubble is truly a bubble, we must examine the hard numbers:
1. **The Capital Requirements:** How much money OpenAI and Anthropic genuinely need to fulfill their commitments.
2. **The Hyperscaler Burden:** How much revenue hyperscalers must extract to pay off their massive infrastructure investments.
3. **VC Exposure:** Venture capital’s true, unhedged exposure to the AI bubble.
4. **The Path to Profitability:** What precisely needs to go right for the math to finally work out.

As this bubble continues to inflate, solving AI's basic economic puzzle grows exponentially harder. Deploying and maintaining infrastructure is becoming vastly more expensive. To achieve profitability, hyperscalers and neoclouds will be forced to charge significantly higher prices for compute—at a time when the only two viable customer segments are fundamentally unable to pay for it.
