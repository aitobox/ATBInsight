# LG to Ban Residential Proxies from Smart TV Apps

## 📌 Executive Summary
LG Electronics USA has announced plans to suspend webOS smart TV apps that incorporate residential proxy Software Development Kits (SDKs). This decision follows a security investigation by Spur, which revealed that over 42% of LG smart TV apps and 25% of Samsung Tizen apps utilized SDKs to turn consumer televisions into continuous proxy nodes for third-party internet traffic routing. While proxy providers like Bright Data defend their opt-in models, security experts warn that smart TVs are poorly suited for proxy integration due to insufficient oversight and risks regarding household consent.

---

## 🔍 Background: The Rise of Smart TV Proxies
Recent research by the security firm **Spur** exposed the widespread presence of residential proxy SDKs embedded within smart television applications. These SDKs—bundled into various applications ranging from simple games like *Pac-Man* to screensavers and file utilities—allow developers to monetize their creations by routing third-party traffic through consumer IP addresses.

* **LG webOS Impact:** Over 42% of apps contained proxy SDKs.
* **Samsung Tizen Impact:** More than 25% of apps contained similar components.
* **Primary Providers:** Spur’s report noted that networks like **Bright Data** accounted for the majority of these SDK implementations.

---

## 🛠️ LG’s Enforcement and Response
In response to these findings, LG Senior Vice President **John Taylor** stated that residential proxy networks do not align with the intended use of LG smart TVs. 

* **Developer Directive:** LG is actively working with app developers to remove residential proxy options.
* **Consequences:** Non-compliant developers will face application suspension from the webOS platform.
* **Evaluation Updates:** LG is currently reviewing existing apps and strengthening its screening processes for newly submitted software.

---

## 🗣️ Industry Perspectives

### The Proxy Providers' Stance
Bright Data defended its operations, emphasizing transparency, user consent, and security compliance:
> *"Every peer opts in through a dedicated screen and receives value in return; every customer is vetted, and our practices have now undergone a second independent audit by PwC."*

Proxy companies maintain that they enforce strict Know-Your-Customer (KYC) protocols and employ technical countermeasures to prevent users from accessing or interacting with other devices on a proxy host's local network.

### The Security Concerns
Security researchers argue that smart TVs are fundamentally problematic environments for residential proxies:
* **Inadequate Oversight:** Consumers rarely view smart TVs as traditional computers capable of being audited.
* **Consent Issues:** A single, one-time consent prompt can easily be accepted by unqualified household members, such as minors.

---

## 🖥️ Additional Controversy: LG Monitors and Unsolicited Software
Alongside the webOS proxy crackdown, LG recently faced criticism regarding software bundling on its hardware. Investigations by the YouTube channel *Gamers Nexus* revealed that certain high-end LG LCD monitors automatically install an app via Windows Update without explicit user approval, which aggressively promotes paid McAfee antivirus subscriptions.
