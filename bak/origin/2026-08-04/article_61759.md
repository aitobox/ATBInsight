# AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decision-Making

### Summary
In this insightful piece, Nik Suresh explores the current corporate obsession with Artificial Intelligence, revealing a landscape where performative adoption often outweighs actual utility. Through a series of candid anecdotes, the article highlights how the pressure to appear "AI-first" is distorting professional judgment, fueling technical theater, and creating a culture of silence where skepticism is treated as a career-ending heresy.

---

### The Theater of AI Adoption
The current climate has created a bizarre disconnect between executive strategy and operational reality. Suresh shares several striking examples of this phenomenon:

*   **The Strategy Gap:** One executive, responsible for a technical strategy at a $2B+ revenue organization, admitted they had never actually used ChatGPT or any AI tool, despite their entire roadmap being centered around the technology.
*   **The "Keep My Job" Workflow:** An engineer at a company obsessed with AI token leaderboards described a cynical workflow: rewriting perfectly functional Go code into Zig simply to justify their role and satisfy the company's mandate to "use AI."

### The "Heresy" of Skepticism
Perhaps the most concerning revelation is why this cycle of exaggeration continues unchecked. Suresh recounts a conversation with a skeptical executive who explained that honesty has become a liability:

> "Executives at their customers were saying absurd things about achieving 100x productivity, and this meant that if any executive at the vendor said that these gains were not plausible, it would undermine the credibility of the customer’s executive, be perceived as an attack (or heresy), and possibly result in an enterprise contract cancellation."

In this environment, challenging the AI narrative is not just unpopular—it is a direct threat to business relationships. Consequently, vendors and clients alike participate in a mutual delusion, prioritizing the preservation of contracts over the reality of technical limitations.
