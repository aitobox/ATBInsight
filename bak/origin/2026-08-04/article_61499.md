# MG Siegler: “OpenAI Makes ChatGPT ChatGPT Again”

## Summary
In a recent critique on *Spyglass*, MG Siegler examines OpenAI’s recent, highly controversial product rollout that merged ChatGPT and Codex. While Siegler argues that OpenAI fundamentally misunderstands its user base, this commentary goes a step further: the rollout wasn't just a misstep—it was a colossal blunder driven by AI researchers who don't understand consumer software design, failing to grasp why simple products (like ChatGPT or iMovie) must remain separate from complex power tools (like Codex or Final Cut Pro).

---

## The Core Dilemma: A Disconnected Product Strategy

Writing at [*Spyglass*](https://spyglass.org/openai-chatgpt-gpt-5-backlash/), MG Siegler highlights the friction of OpenAI’s recent update:

> In that light, we can see the dilemma. But there were also probably about a dozen better ways to do this roll-out — as highlighted by how fast they’re fixing these things — and they just missed the mark. While they undoubtedly knew there would be some backlash, they probably didn’t realize there would be *this much*. Again. All that points to perhaps [a disturbing trend](https://spyglass.org/openai-chatgpt-gpt-5-backlash/) where OpenAI doesn’t really understand their user base. 
>
> Which you almost can understand given how they clearly stumbled into ChatGPT in the first place. Still, here they are with those billion users. A problem that all of their competitors would love to have. But still a problem if you want to fundamentally [change what you are as a product](https://spyglass.org/openai-product-launches/).
>
> But those are bigger, existential issues. All I wanted was chat back in the ChatGPT app. Front and center. And I got it. It’s not perfect. It’s still bloated. But at least it’s usable again now and not a confusing mess of ideas out of the box.

## A Deeper Misunderstanding

While Siegler’s take is scathing, it arguably lets OpenAI off too easy. There was fundamentally nothing wrong with the previous architecture: two distinct apps—simple **ChatGPT** and complex **Codex**. (Granted, the Mac version of Codex being an Electron app was a flaw, but that complaint feels quaint now.)

Adding “ChatGPT” as a tab inside Codex is fine. Renaming Codex to “ChatGPT” is just stupid. OpenAI has *not* made ChatGPT feel like ChatGPT again.

There is not one single tiny feature in the new ChatGPT app that provides an incentive to switch away from **ChatGPT Classic**. Meanwhile, ChatGPT Classic retains features that simply don't exist in the new app—or are markedly worse. For example, deleting a chat requires archiving it first, then hunting down an archive folder to complete the deletion.

### The Real Problem: Eggheads Running Product

The issue isn't merely that OpenAI “doesn’t understand their user base.” The root cause is that product decisions are now being driven by AI research eggheads who don’t understand their own products. They look at [confusing interface updates](https://daringfireball.net/misc/2026/07/chatgpt-updates.png) and genuinely think they make sense. 

Consider the current fractured state of the ecosystem:
* **iOS and Android:** Still run the old, familiar ChatGPT.
* **Mac (Old):** Relegated to “ChatGPT Classic.”
* **Mac (New):** An entirely different, orders-of-magnitude more complicated app simply named “ChatGPT.”

It makes no sense. 

## The Simple Solution

The straightforward fix is to pretend the last week never happened:
1. Revert to calling the simple chat interface **ChatGPT**.
2. Keep the advanced developer interface as **Codex**. 

If a rebrand is truly necessary, don’t demote the original to “ChatGPT Classic”—instead, rename Codex to **“ChatGPT Codex.”** Problem solved.

## The Apple Analogy: Consumer vs. Pro Software

The Codex app is undeniably capable of amazing things. However, [OpenAI’s own accounting](https://openai.com/index/codex-for-knowledge-work/) shows that while ChatGPT boasts a billion users, Codex has only a few million. 

Why? Because ChatGPT is simple, focused, and built around a single, coherent concept: **chat**. 

The frustration among the researchers now running product at OpenAI is palpable: *Why aren't the hundreds of millions of people using ChatGPT running Codex instead?* Their misguided solution was to slap the “ChatGPT” name onto Codex. 

To put it in perspective, this is equivalent to Apple deleting the **Messages** app on the Mac, replacing it with **Xcode**, and renaming Xcode to “Messages.” Then, as an afterthought, they embed an “iMessage” tab in the Xcode sidebar and re-release the original Messages app as “Messages Classic.”

OpenAI previously separated itself from competitors like Anthropic by excelling at product design. Tragically, their product decisions are now being made by people who fail to understand why Apple maintains separate tiers for consumer and pro software—like *iMovie* and *Final Cut Pro*, or *GarageBand* and *Logic Pro*.
