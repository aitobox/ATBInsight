# Dario Takes It on the Chin

## 📋 Summary
Public sentiment toward tech leaders is shifting, with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei facing a recent wave of backlash. Following controversies over open-weight models, tone-deaf policy statements, and emerging market dominance concerns, Amodei's goodwill is evaporating—much like Sam Altman's before him. While public polls suggest users slightly prefer Amodei over Altman, the vast majority ultimately distrust both figures leading the multi-trillion-dollar AI revolution.

---

## The Rising Backlash Against Anthropic

Remember the essay from March? A lot of people have come around to that view:

[![Industry Backlash](./images/112d37723f59.png)](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/a-backlash-against-anthropic-is-brewing-in-silicon-valley-3b3ddc80?st=E4P3sc&reflink=article_email_share)

You can read the full [Wall Street Journal story here](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/a-backlash-against-anthropic-is-brewing-in-silicon-valley-3b3ddc80?st=E4P3sc&reflink=article_email_share). It reviews a wide range of concerns—from business practices and accusations of overdone doomerism to suspicions that Anthropic is trying to kneecap its competitors. 

The turning point for many seems to be events from the past few days. While much of the AI industry enthusiastically backed "open-weight" models (which can be downloaded given sufficient hardware, though not fully open-source since training sets remain private), Amodei resisted. Instead, he issued a statement explaining his stance that many critics viewed as tone-deaf and self-serving. 

This friction was compounded by simultaneous reports that [Anthropic has been destroying rare books in bulk to distill their contents](https://x.com/hedgiemarkets/status/2081534588485296565?s=61), even as Amodei's statement called for locking down distillation by competitors.

## Altman vs. Amodei: Two Sides of the Same Trust Deficit

I found myself (somewhat unusually) heartily agreeing with David Sacks. All told, it appears that most of the goodwill Amodei accumulated by standing up to Pete Hegseth has dissipated, much like the goodwill once afforded to Sam Altman. 

Perhaps one or both of them will eventually regain the public's trust, but it is far from guaranteed:
* **Sam Altman:** The core issue is a lack of candor and honesty; people simply don't trust him anymore, and for good reason.
* **Dario Amodei:** While he arguably means well, the issue stems from tone-deafness, self-certainty, and a tendency to present arguments that frequently appear self-serving.

In honor of these contrasting leadership styles—and with apologies for the AI slop—I made a meme. Coming soon: *a guy who could sell refrigerators to Eskimos (probably lying about their future benefits)* versus *a guy who often seems unable to read a room*:

[![The Battle for the Soul of AI Meme](./images/e34bb5c3c435.png)](./images/e34bb5c3c435.png)
*_THE BATTLE FOR THE SOUL OF AI_*

More seriously, the *WSJ* article also points out a broader structural worry: 
> *"Technologists and policymakers say they are also concerned about the AI race turning into a duopoly, with Anthropic and OpenAI essentially emerging as dominant players that control the market."*

Given how damaging AI could ultimately be, we need leaders we can fully trust. Right now, it doesn't feel like we have them.
