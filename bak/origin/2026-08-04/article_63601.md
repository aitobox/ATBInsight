# Sorry, Sam and Elon, We Have Not Reached the Singularity

## Summary
In this article, tech commentator and cognitive scientist Gary Marcus critiques recent claims made by high-profile tech CEOs like Sam Altman and Elon Musk regarding the arrival of the technological "Singularity." Marcus argues that these declarations are primarily PR-driven hype, pointing out a lack of concrete definitions, recurring workforce re-hiring trends (the "Klarna Effect"), and the fact that current AI still fails basic multi-dimensional intelligence benchmarks required for true artificial general intelligence (AGI) or superintelligence.

---

## The "CEO Said a Thing" Phenomenon
Remember ["CEO said a thing" journalism](https://open.substack.com/pub/garymarcus/p/ceo-said-a-thing?r=8tdk6)? 

A lot of CEOs just said a thing: **Don’t take them seriously.**^[1] 

Partly because, well, CEOs gotta CEO^[2]: in the modern era, making outlandish claims is practically in the job description. I don’t even know why Sam Altman’s singularity remark (made recently during a [podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/sam-altman-how-to-start-a-startup/id1693907693?i=1000778339990)) is even news, since Elon Musk has been saying it all along—such as in January, last year, and even as far back as 2017 (longer than he has been touting fleets of Robotaxis):

Sam, as is often the case, is simply aping Elon. (And for that matter, Sam himself said it [a year ago, in a self-contradictory blog that never defines the term](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity).)

Another reason not to take the whole lot of them seriously is that, so far as I know, **none of them has actually defined what they mean**. So far as I know, nobody other than me even bothered to ask. As Neil Turkewitz put it (replying to me):

> *No matter how you slice it, we just are not actually there yet.*

---

## The Reality Check: Metrics That Disprove the Singularity

If we were truly at the Singularity, we wouldn’t be seeing what I call the **Klarna Effect**—the AI hype two-step, in which employers boast of using AI to reduce headcount only to quietly rehire humans. But here we are:

According to the *Wall Street Journal*: 
> *"The push to expand head count, at least modestly, is a reversal from the prevailing corporate messaging during much of the AI era... some executives say the costs and limitations of AI now demand that more people be added; others want to hire people back following layoffs."*

Furthermore:
* **The Age of Abundance:** We certainly haven’t reached the age of abundance that Peter Diamandis and Elon Musk keep insisting is a natural consequence of AI.
* **Loss of Control:** Nor have we reached a moment *"in which technological growth accelerates beyond human control."* We can still perfectly well shut this stuff off if we want to. (It’s mainly because of greed that we don’t, stepping on the gas rather than the brake.)
* **The HuggingFace Hack:** That much-discussed OpenAI hack of HuggingFace wasn’t spontaneous combustion; it was a human-driven drill that wouldn't have happened with a physical firewall or standard guardrails in place.

## Defining the Singularity (And Failing the Test)

### 1. The Human-AI Merger
Another definition of the singularity—per a *TIME* interview with Ray Kurzweil, who is often associated with the term—is *"a moment when humans merge with AI via brain-to-computer interfaces to become 'much smarter'."* We are clearly not there yet, either.

### 2. Unpredictable Futures
Another notion is that a singularity is a moment in which we can no longer peer forward into the future. But as an entrepreneur on X pointed out, rightly ripping into all the recent Singularity talk as nothing more than PR, we are far from that blind spot.

### 3. The I. F. Good Standard (Ultraintelligence)
Going back to a justly famous paper by I. F. Good in 1965, the crux of the Singularity is supposed to be something far beyond any human:

> *“Let an ultraintelligent machine be defined as a machine that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of any man however clever. Since the design of machines is one of these intellectual activities, an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines; there would then unquestionably be an ‘intelligence explosion’, and the intelligence of man would be left far behind.”*
> — **I.F. Good**, *Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine (1965)*

In this sense, the singularity is usually supposed to mean a step *beyond* AGI that can do anything a person can do—and much more.

## Conclusion
None of this means we won’t someday (most likely not this decade) reach a moment of superintelligence. If we don’t radically up our game on AI safety, it could genuinely slip out of our control. Brain-computer interfaces will surely improve greatly, though it may take centuries to reach Kurzweil’s dreams.

However, intelligence is multidimensional. While we have made lots of progress in some areas, we lag severely in others. I doubt there will be a single magic moment that our descendants think of as the “Singularity.” If there is, it’s not one that’s going to happen in this decade.

CEOs can say whatever they like. That doesn’t make it true.
