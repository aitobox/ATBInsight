# Google and Epic Cease Hostilities: Third-Party Android App Stores Arrive Next Week

### Summary
In a significant shift for the mobile ecosystem, Google has withdrawn its motion to modify the U.S. court’s permanent injunction regarding the Google Play Store. This move signals an end to the immediate legal standoff with Epic Games, paving the way for third-party app stores to integrate with the Android ecosystem starting July 22nd.

***

### The End of the Legal Standoff
Reporting for *The Verge*, Sean Hollister notes that Google has opted to abandon its challenge to Judge Donato’s injunction to avoid further uncertainty. In an official statement, Google spokesperson Dan Jackson stated:

> "We’ve agreed with Epic to withdraw our motion... This allows us to focus on executing our recently announced global business model evolution to deliver greater app store choice, lower prices, and more opportunities for developers and users."

Google emphasized that it remains committed to maintaining Android’s security standards while complying with the court's mandate to foster a more competitive environment.

### The Path Forward: Two Tracks for Android
The resolution of this conflict highlights a bifurcated future for Android distribution:
*   **United States:** The implementation of "stores-within-a-store," allowing third-party platforms to operate under the court's injunction.
*   **Global:** The rollout of Google’s "Registered App Store" program, which is slated to launch with the next major version of Android later this year.

### Implementation and Developer Impact
Google has begun notifying U.S. developers that their app and game listings will be made available to third-party stores by default starting **July 22nd**, unless they proactively opt out. To facilitate this transition, Google has launched the [Play Catalog Access Program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17117200?hl=en), providing a portal for third-party stores to enroll and access the catalog.

### Commentary
The resolution raises questions regarding the "no criticism" gag order previously agreed upon by Epic CEO Tim Sweeney in exchange for an $800 million settlement earlier this year. 

As the July 22nd deadline approaches, the industry is watching closely to see how developers respond. While major app developers are expected to opt out of third-party distribution to maintain control, the gaming sector may prove more willing to experiment with these new, open channels.
