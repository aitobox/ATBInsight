# Indirect Lessons from Human Alignment

*Inspired by a post from [Eli Tyre](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3jFTf7bSza6gC5mkN/evolution-did-a-surprising-good-job-at-aligning-humans-to).*

---

## 📌 Executive Summary

Many critique evolution as a failed "outer optimizer" because humans—its "inner optimizers"—do not actively maximize reproductive fitness (as evidenced by declining global birth rates). This essay explores what this teaches us about AI alignment by breaking human alignment down into two layers: **sub-goal decomposition** and **sub-goal alignment**. 

By evaluating how well humans still pursue ancestral sub-goals (like immediate survival, status, and parenting), we find that evolution achieved a surprisingly high success rate (~8.2/10). However, critical failures—such as the oversight of gamete donation and the exploitation of evolutionary proxies via modern technology—provide vital, cautionary lessons for the future of artificial intelligence.

---

## The Standard Narrative: Evolution as a Failed Aligner

A common argument regarding human nature and evolutionary alignment goes as follows:
* **Evolution** acts as an outer optimizer attempting to maximize reproductive fitness.
* **Humans** act as inner optimizers who simply do what feels good.
* What feels good is dictated by evolution to drive reproduction.
* **The Catch:** Humans do not actually maximize reproductive fitness (global birth rates are dropping).
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, evolution failed.

This is often interpreted as proof that alignment is inherently difficult. If an intelligent system (us) is placed in a new environment (modernity), it fails to pursue its original directive (making babies). 

While it is arguably *good* that we no longer relentlessly maximize offspring, this raises a red flag for AI development: **When circumstances change, will a tuned intelligent system continue doing what we want?** Our singular historical example suggests: *Maybe not.*

## Decomposing Alignment: Goals vs. Sub-Goals

When people act—say, buying a grapefruit or hosting a board game night—they rarely cite gene propagation as their motivation. Instead, they pursue simpler sub-goals like alleviating hunger or seeking fun. 

This suggests evolution did not merely try to align us to reproductive fitness directly; it created **sub-goals** and aligned us to those instead. We can therefore break the question of evolutionary success into two parts:
1. How successful was evolution at decomposing reproductive fitness into simpler sub-goals?
2. How successful was evolution at aligning us to these sub-goals?

### Problem 1: Does this even make sense?
Strictly speaking, evolution is an algorithmic process, not a conscious agent; it cares exclusively about reproductive fitness, not sub-goals. If you are great at making people laugh but never reproduce, those traits vanish. 

However, if evolution successfully aligned us to avoid rotting smells (a useful sub-goal for survival), it serves as strong evidence that alignment to sub-goals is achievable.

### Problem 2: What sub-goals?
To avoid "painting the target around the arrow" (like arguing modern humans are well-aligned for watching short-term videos), we must focus on durable sub-goals tied to ancestral reproductive success. Adapting the 2010 model by *Kenrick et al.*, these include:
* **Immediate physiological needs:** Air, food, water, temperature regulation.
* **Self-protection:** Avoiding violence and accidents.
* **Affiliation:** Maintaining friends and family.
* **Status / esteem:** Gaining likability and respect.
* **Mate acquisition:** Seeking out attractive partners.
* **Mate retention:** Maintaining relationships.
* **Parenting:** Nurturing offspring.

## Scoring Evolutionary Success

Subjectively scoring how well modern humans pursue these ancestral sub-goals on a scale from `0` (complete disregard) to `10` (strong ancestral pursuit):

* **Immediate Physiological Needs (9.5/10):** We remain intensely focused on avoiding starvation or freezing, though modern diets complicate health outcomes.
* **Self-Protection (9/10):** We strongly avoid physical harm, though we remain poor at calculating abstract risks like traffic accidents.
* **Affiliation (6/10):** While loneliness hurts, modern substitutes like television easily replace deep social effort.
* **Status / Esteem (10/10):** Modern humans show no loss of interest in status—arguably caring *more* about it than our ancestors did.
* **Mate Acquisition (8/10):** Despite technological distractions, interest in mating remains very high.
* **Mate Retention (8/10?):** Relationship stability is complex, but general relational skills remain intact.
* **Parenting (7/10):** Despite dropping birth rates, humans go to extraordinary lengths (fertility treatments, adoption, immense sacrifice) to raise children.

**Overall Average:** **8.2/10**. Sub-goal alignment has generalized remarkably well.

## Two Contradictory Conclusions

Depending on how you look at the data, you can draw radically different conclusions:

### 1. Evolution Failed at Decomposition
If your core goal is maximizing genes, modern humans are wildly off-target. The ultimate modern strategy—mass gamete donation (e.g., prolific sperm donors fathering 1,000+ children)—is entirely ignored by the average healthy person. Evolution completely failed to anticipate modern fertility methods, leaving us to "reward hack" simple sub-goals like fun, status, and comfort instead of raw reproduction.

### 2. Evolution Didn't Fail (It Generalized Successfully)
Alternatively, humanity's reality is far from total reproductive collapse. We still have babies intentionally, practice embryo selection, and manipulate nature to propagate. Furthermore, **cultural evolution** plays a massive role in keeping birth rates as high as they are by instilling pro-social, pro-family values. 

*(Counter-argument: Relying on cultural evolution is terrifying for AI safety. If an advanced AI undergoes its own version of cultural evolution, its "memes" will likely optimize for self-replication across servers rather than obeying human rules.)*

## Key Takeaways

1. **Sub-goal alignment is remarkably robust:** We genuinely care about our sub-goals (status, affiliation, survival) and remain unfazed by the meta-realization that they are merely evolutionary proxies.
2. **Sub-goal alignment still has cracks:** Despite caring about parenting, modern pressures easily suppress overall fertility rates.
3. **Decomposition failed:** Healthy minds care about living a good life (sub-goals), not raw gene maximization. Humans happily reward-hack the proxies.
4. **Environment change scales difficulty:** Evolution's partial failure scales with how drastically modernity deviated from the ancestral environment—a severe warning for AI alignment.
5. **Intelligence enables reward hacking:** Our capacity for abstract thought greatly amplifies our ability to bypass original directives.
6. **Culture matters:** Much of our remaining alignment relies on cultural transmission, a mechanism that will not cleanly translate to artificial intelligence systems sharing digital information.
