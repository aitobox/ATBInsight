# Apple Sues OpenAI, io, and Former Employees, Alleging Theft of Trade Secrets

## Executive Summary

Apple has filed a major lawsuit against OpenAI, hardware startup io, and several former Apple employees—including high-profile departures Tang Tan and Chang Liu. The lawsuit alleges a coordinated, institutional-level theft of Apple’s trade secrets and confidential information to jumpstart OpenAI's consumer hardware ambitions, which are being led by former Apple Chief Design Officer Jony Ive. Despite the ongoing commercial integration of ChatGPT into Apple Intelligence, legal analysts note that this explosive litigation makes the future of the partnership practically untenable.

---

## Key Defendants and Connections

The lawsuit targets a network of former Apple executives and the corporate entities now employing them:

* **Tang Tan:** Former VP of Product Design at Apple (overseeing iPhone and Apple Watch), who departed in February 2024 to join Jony Ive.
* **Chang Liu:** Former senior system electrical engineer at Apple with an eight-year tenure, who left in January 2026 to join OpenAI.
* **OpenAI & io Products:** OpenAI acquired Jony Ive’s startup, **io**, in a massive $6.5 billion deal that brought over 50 former Apple engineers and developers into the fold. 
* **Other Notable Figures:** io was founded by Tan alongside former Apple design leaders **Jony Ive**, **Evans Hankey**, and **Scott Cannon**. Interestingly, while Ive, Hankey, and Cannon are not named as individual defendants in the initial filing, the complaint explicitly points to their venture as part of the coordinated misconduct.

---

## Inside Apple’s Allegations

According to the legal complaint [hosted here](https://daringfireball.net/misc/2026/06/Apple_Inc._v._Chang_Liu_et_al.pdf), Apple accuses the defendants of systematically exploiting proprietary information to infiltrate the consumer hardware market:

> "OpenAI and its cohorts have been engaging in a coordinated pattern of misconduct at an institutional level as well. This includes io (which OpenAI acquired), *a venture co-founded by Mr. Tan and other former Apple leaders*... 
> 
> They have used confidential Apple information in approaching Apple’s trusted partners, even having one carry out a specific trade secret metal-finishing technique for OpenAI, misleading the partner to believe they had Apple’s permission to do so."

The complaint paints a severe picture of OpenAI's hardware division:

> "Apple lacks visibility into what’s been happening behind closed doors at OpenAI, where such misconduct is normalized and exemplified by leadership... OpenAI has been stealing Apple’s trade secrets and confidential information. As a natural result, OpenAI’s nascent hardware business now rests on the shakiest of foundations, rotten to its core by its illegal reliance on misappropriated trade secrets."

---

## The Impact on the Apple-OpenAI Partnership

A notable technicality is addressed in **Footnote 13 (page 15)** of the filing, which clarifies the boundary between the lawsuit and existing business agreements:

> "Apple and OpenAI have a commercial relationship involving the integration of OpenAI’s ChatGPT into Apple Intelligence. The companies have entered into a written agreement governing that integration. That agreement is not at issue here. OpenAI’s acts of trade secret misappropriation alleged herein do not arise from and have no connection to that agreement."

However, industry observers point out that regardless of the legal separation of these contracts, maintaining the Apple Intelligence partnership in practical terms appears highly unlikely following such a severe legal confrontation.
