# Breaking: Demis Hassabis Endorses Preflight Safety Testing for AI

## 📌 Executive Summary
Google DeepMind CEO Sir Demis Hassabis has publicly thrown his support behind a version of preflight safety testing for large-scale AI models. This endorsement marks a significant turning point in AI policy, aligning with long-standing advocacy for FDA-style or FINRA-style regulatory frameworks to ensure safer, more transparent AI deployments.

---

## Background: The Long Road to Preflight Testing
Since 2023, preflight safety testing for large-scale AI models has been championed across multiple platforms—including [2023 Senate testimony](https://www.techpolicy.press/transcript-senate-judiciary-subcommittee-hearing-on-oversight-of-ai/), [essays on AI oversight](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/is-it-time-to-hit-the-pause-button?r=8tdk6), and the 2024 book [*Taming Silicon Valley*](https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/5828/Taming-Silicon-ValleyHow-We-Can-Ensure-That-AI?__cf_chl_rt_tk=GrWhD9iiX7vQLMUUogReFS4qOD96mYmeWP5gA7Ee6kM-1784038139-1.0.1.1-CW2Tds3gTBgFLXlgS0gPKwqGAvyT1keqMHSGap7hmxo). 

The core proposal has always been modeled after pharmaceutical evaluations (like the FDA) or financial oversight: a mandatory, transparent, and independent system to analyze the costs and benefits of advanced models prior to widespread deployment.

While initial Senate receptions in 2023 were positive, momentum stalled amid shifting political landscapes and a widespread hostility toward AI regulation in Washington following the Trump administration's transition. However, recent developments—such as the "Mythos moment"—prompted the White House to introduce preliminary preflight measures, albeit lacking the crucial elements of complete transparency, independence, breadth, and mandatoriness.

---

## A Major Endorsement from DeepMind
The landscape shifted significantly when Google DeepMind CEO **Sir Demis Hassabis** [publicly endorsed a version of preflight safety testing](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2076957440109625718?s=61). 

[![Demis Hassabis statement reference](./images/a7d17bb43e8b.png)](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2076957440109625718?s=61)

Hassabis outlined a pragmatic pathway for implementation:

> *"Initially, Frontier Labs would voluntarily share models with the Standards Body for review up to 30 days before release. Once the assessment protocol is shown to be effective and robust, formalisation could quickly follow, meaning that Frontier Models would be required to pass it to be deployed in the US market. Labs would also work with the Standards Body to address any critical post-release vulnerabilities."*

---

## Why Independence Matters
A critical takeaway from Hassabis's proposal is the emphasis on involving **independent technical experts**. 

Decisions regarding AI safety cannot be left solely in the hands of governments and big tech monopolies. This is especially vital given potential conflicts of interest, such as instances where governments contemplate taking direct financial stakes in AI companies. 

Read [Demis Hassabis's full essay here](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2076957440109625718?s=61). Adopting a FINRA-style model with built-in transparency and independence will make the world a safer place, and this brave essay could very well serve as a historic turning point for global AI governance.
