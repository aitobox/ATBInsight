# Impro: A Handbook for Running a Cult

### Summary
Keith Johnstone’s *Impro* is widely regarded as a seminal text on improvisation, yet its influence extends far beyond the stage. Frequently cited by Silicon Valley founders and tech leaders, the book serves as a guide for unlocking human creativity by dismantling societal repression. However, beneath its practical advice on status and performance lies a more unsettling reality: *Impro* functions as a blueprint for charismatic leadership and the psychological molding of disciples—essentially, a handbook for running a cult.

---

### The Core Thesis: Unlearning Repression
Johnstone’s philosophy rests on three pillars:
1. **Natural Creativity:** Children are inherently creative, but Western culture and education systematically repress this nature.
2. **The Unlearning Process:** Becoming truly expressive requires stripping away the habits of adult repression.
3. **Improv as a Way of Life:** Improvisation is not merely an acting technique; it is a mechanism for achieving an "authentic" human existence.

### Status Games and Social Dynamics
The most practical takeaway from *Impro* for professionals—particularly software engineers—is Johnstone’s analysis of **status**. 
* **Pervasiveness:** Status games are present in every social interaction, from casual conversation to workplace hierarchy.
* **The Skill of Switching:** Effective social interaction requires the ability to fluidly shift between high and low status. A person who can only perform one is either a bully or a victim; a master of status can navigate any room by knowing when to lead and when to defer.

### The Cultish Undercurrent
While the status advice is useful, the book’s later chapters—specifically those regarding "Masks and Trance"—reveal a darker, more manipulative methodology. Johnstone describes techniques designed to induce trance states, bypass the "personality," and encourage students to surrender their sense of self to the teacher. 

He explicitly positions himself as a "guru" who grants permission for forbidden thoughts. By framing the imagination as the "true self" and absolving students of responsibility for their actions, he creates a psychological environment where disciples become highly suggestible and dependent on his guidance.

### Startups and the "Cult" Parallel
The popularity of *Impro* in Silicon Valley is no coincidence. As Peter Thiel noted, successful startups often function as "slightly less extreme kinds of cults." 
* **The Appeal:** For founders, the book provides a framework for building teams that disregard traditional norms, think on their feet, and align completely with the leader's vision.
* **The Warning:** For employees, *Impro* serves as a diagnostic tool. If a company demands you "reinvent your personality" or encourages you to enter a "trance-like" state of unquestioning productivity, you may be in a cult, not a workplace.

### Conclusion: The Value of Shackles
While Johnstone’s insights into creativity and power dynamics are undeniably sharp, his life philosophy is a dangerous one to adopt. The "shackles" of social norms—such as maintaining professional boundaries and avoiding the exploitation of vulnerable students—exist for a reason. 

*Impro* remains a valuable text for understanding performance and power, but it should be read with a healthy dose of skepticism. Most of us are not destined to be enlightened gurus; we are ordinary people who thrive best within the protective, boring, and necessary structures of a society governed by shared norms.
