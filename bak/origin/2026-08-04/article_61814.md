# 9to5Mac Uncovers Dozens of Disguised Gambling Apps on the App Store in Brazil

## Summary
An investigation by *9to5Mac* revealed over 60 "jacket apps"—Trojan Horse-style applications disguised as harmless utilities or games (such as weather and navigation tools)—that hide online gambling platforms when accessed from Brazilian IP addresses. Notably, one such app even climbed to the #1 spot in the Brazilian App Store's Weather category. While initial review misses are understandable, critics argue that Apple's oversight failed by allowing such glaringly miscategorized apps to achieve mainstream popularity. Apple has since responded, removing several apps and terminating associated developer accounts.

---

## The Investigation

According to [*9to5Mac*](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/07/19/9to5mac-brazil-gambling-apps):

> Brazilian users browsing the App Store rankings in categories such as Navigation, Travel, and Weather have noticed a growing number of poorly made games appearing among the top results, many of them featuring AI-generated illustrations of animals as their app icons.
> 
> As it turns out, these are so-called jacket apps, which are just a front for hidden betting and gambling apps. A *9to5Mac* investigation has uncovered more than 60 apps that behave exactly as depicted in their App Store screenshots when accessed from virtually anywhere in the world, except Brazil.

## The Flaw in App Review and the Need for a "Bunco Squad"

Obviously, the main problem is that these apps disguise themselves Trojan Horse-style and slip through App Store review undetected. However, expecting App Store review to catch every single attempt is unreasonable; if the disguises are clever enough, some are bound to slip through. That is acceptable.

The issue, however, is that some of these apps are not well disguised at all. What sense does it make that "PotatoPlot Puzzle"—a game featuring a rabbit potato farmer—is listed as a *weather* app? How is that not an immediate red flag?

Furthermore, this app rose to become not just popular, but the number one weather app in Brazil, a nation of over 200 million citizens. 

In arguments for Apple to commission an internal [bunco squad dedicated to hunting down scammers](https://daringfireball.net/search/bunco+squad), the goal shouldn't be to make the App Store 100% scammer-free (an impossible standard destined for failure). Instead, the focus should be on keeping the App Store free of *successful* scams—targeting the most popular and lucrative apps. There is simply no feasible excuse for "PotatoPlot Puzzle" to have become the top "weather app" in a major market like Brazil.

## Apple's Response

> **Update:** [Apple issued a statement to *9to5Mac*](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/17/investigation-reveals-dozens-of-disguised-gambling-apps-on-the-app-store-in-brazil/#:~:text=Update%20July%2020):
> 
> The company thanked *9to5Mac* for its investigation and added that, prior to the reporting, it had already investigated and taken action against 25 similar apps, while also terminating the developer accounts associated with them.
