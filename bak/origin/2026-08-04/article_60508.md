# Pluralistic: Why aren't AI companies competing directly with their customers?

**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Source:** [Pluralistic.net](https://pluralistic.net/2026/07/13/go-meta-meta/)

---

### Summary
In this edition of *Pluralistic*, Cory Doctorow explores the logical inconsistency of the modern AI industry. He poses a provocative question: If AI companies truly possess technology capable of replacing human labor—from doctors to accountants—why are they selling these tools to existing businesses rather than simply launching their own, more efficient firms? Doctorow argues that this behavior is a symptom of a "go meta" economy, where the most profitable business model is not to perform useful work, but to sell the "picks and shovels" to those who do, effectively distancing themselves from the risks of actual production.

---

### The "Go Meta" Economy
Doctorow draws on the classic adage, "In a gold rush, the only people who come out ahead are the ones selling the picks and shovels." He suggests that today’s AI "hucksters" have taken this a step further: they aren't just selling the tools; they are selling the *promise* of infinite wealth to anyone willing to pay, while avoiding the messy reality of running a functional business.

*   **The Khayyám Question:** Referencing *The Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám*, Doctorow asks: "What do the AI hucksters buy/One half so precious as the Gods they sell?"
*   **The Logic Gap:** If an AI can perform a doctor's job more cheaply than a human, the AI company should logically open its own hospital to capture the full profit margin. By selling the tool to a hospital instead, they are choosing a "meta" layer of abstraction rather than the actual service.
*   **The Scam Economy:** This trend mirrors the world of online grifters who peddle tools to generate "get-rich-quick" schemes rather than engaging in the actual work of wealth creation.
