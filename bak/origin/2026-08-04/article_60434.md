# I Love LLMs, I Hate Hype

> **Summary:** 
> The author expresses profound enthusiasm for the genuine technological progress in artificial intelligence—ranging from new LLMs and self-driving cars to video generation and local coding agents—while fiercely criticizing the pervasive negative hype, elitist doomsday cultism, and overinflated valuations of frontier labs. Ultimately, AI is viewed not as a magical singularity controlled by a privileged few, but as the natural, commodified continuation of the computer revolution.

---

## A Genuine Passion for AI
I think from this blog you may misunderestimate how absolutely giddy I am about AI. I did hacking from 2007–2014, and after that my whole [career](https://tinygrad.org/) has been devoted to AI (see also [comma.ai](https://comma.ai/)). I love the progress. I’m so excited for the [new LLMs](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/), [self-driving cars](https://www.tesla.com/fsd), [video generation models](https://github.com/Wan-Video), and [coding agents](https://opencode.ai/). 

I set up a Linux box with `opencode` on my local [GLM-5.2](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2) last week and, wow, just saying `install tmux with the geohot configuration` actually works. The Year of the Linux Desktop is finally here!

## The Problem with Negative Valence Hype
What I don’t like are two things:

1. **Manufactured Anxiety:** This constant bullshit about some window closing, or the perpetual underclass, or falling hopelessly behind. This is *negative valence hype*—not only is it not true, it’s mostly designed to make you feel bad about yourself and move to shitty San Francisco, where everything really does suck just like these people claim.
2. **The Apocalyptic Strawman:** This absurd leap from "oh hey, it’s a fancy autocomplete, smart compiler, better search engine" to "it’s gonna like own the whole light cone, bro." The narrative goes that if you aren’t in SF and at the right parties, there’s gonna be a flash of light in the sky one day and everything will have *Changed* before you even know what happened. 

I’ll bet you everything I have that this doesn’t happen. The people perpetuating this are terrible people, but the poetic justice is that *this is how they feel inside all the time themselves*.

## The Fallacy of Frontier Valuations
Here’s a [cool presentation from 2016](https://idlewords.com/talks/superintelligence.htm) about superintelligence. Here’s a [movie from 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_2:_Judgment_Day) about machines taking over the world. 

A certain cult likes to claim credit for things that are happening with or without them, and this is my main argument against [the valuation of frontier labs](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/06/21/the-doom-justifies-the-valuation.html). It’s not that AI won’t create that much value; it’s that [they won’t profit from it](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/02/19/nobody-will-profit.html).

They try to dress it up with some high-minded safety or geopolitical rhetoric, but the core of the anti-open-source arguments is simply a **fear of commodification**. AI progress is happening mostly due to Moore’s law and general advances in computing, not because of anything special these labs are doing. Of course, they have a strong incentive to hide this from you—otherwise, you might not want to give them billions of dollars.

## Programming in the Age of AI
I might have been a little harsh in [The Eternal Sloptember](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/05/24/the-eternal-sloptember.html) about models not being able to program. What’s really happening is that *programming is changing*. 

Can compilers program? Consider this [Linus Torvalds quote](https://www.reddit.com/r/buildinpublic/comments/1ubja8e/linus_torvalds_people_bragging_about_ai_writing/) noting that while agents might make programming 10x more productive, compilers made programming 1,000x more productive. 

While 10x and 1000x are extreme estimates, I’m now pretty confident I’m getting better at using models and seeing a real productivity boost. It is a brand-new skill, and it's not like [I haven’t constantly been trying them](https://xcancel.com/alxfazio/status/1996207471988486432). 

You have to be really careful—[they can increase cognitive fatigue](https://warpedvisions.org/blog/2025/hitting-the-wall-at-ai-speed/), and all the "vibe-coded" stuff is still slop (seriously, where is all this magical new software that massive productivity improvements should imply?). But models are undeniably useful, serving the same role as find-and-replace, Stack Overflow, or all the regexes I never learned how to write and now never will.

AI is simply the continuation of the computer revolution. I love computers so much.
