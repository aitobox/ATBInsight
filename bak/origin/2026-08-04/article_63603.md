# The More You Buy, The More You Lose

> **Summary:** 
> Big Tech and hyperscalers are pouring trillions of dollars into AI infrastructure, relying on circular financing, massive debt, and skyrocketing capital expenditures that far outstrip actual revenue. Despite grand promises from NVIDIA and tech giants, generative AI remains deeply unprofitable, plagued by supply chain crises, massive debt burdens, and a severe lack of diverse, sustainable market demand. As the financial cracks widen—marked by underperforming bonds, downgraded credit ratings, and a corporate culture built on "AI-washing"—the entire ecosystem is hurtling toward a major financial reckoning.

---

## The Illusion of Efficiency and the Trillion-Dollar Capex Hole

Two years ago, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang famously claimed that “the more you buy, the more you save,” referring to Blackwell GPUs that were supposed to slash LLM inference operating costs and energy use by up to 25x. Two years later, those gains have quietly been pared back to 10x, based on case studies from private, unprofitable inference providers. 

Yet, nobody seems to mind that NVIDIA overstated its gains by 150%. Blackwell being “10x better” hasn’t made any AI startups profitable, nor has it measurably lowered anyone's costs in dollars and cents. Instead, it has resulted in horrendously overstuffed balance sheets and spuriously inflated stock prices.

By the end of 2026, hyperscalers will have sunk over **$1.3 trillion** into generative AI, with plans to spend a trillion more the following year. On any rational level, LLMs cannot possibly bring in the **$2 trillion+ in brand-new revenue** required to make this spending worthwhile. 

Between March 2022 and July 2026:
* **Meta, Google, Amazon, and Microsoft** added over $850 billion in property, plant, and equipment (PP&E), nearly tripling it from $498 billion.
* Capital expenditures now eat up massive portions of revenue: **43.4% at Google**, **37.3% at Microsoft**, **33.7% at Meta**, and **24.4% at Amazon**.
* On-balance sheet debt for these giants doubled from $250 billion to **$557 billion**.
* Off-balance sheet debt—such as unfulfilled data center commitments and Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs)—stands at a staggering **$1.35 trillion**.

## The Vicious Cycle of Debt and Memory Shortages

As hyperscalers become repeat visitors to the bond markets to fund their capex, investor confidence is curdling. Google’s recent century bond has already lost 10% of its value, and Bloomberg Intelligence estimates that hyperscalers will need to raise **$1.5 trillion** in investment-grade debt over the next five years just to keep pace. 

Meanwhile, secondary players and neoclouds are bleeding out:
* **CoreWeave** has raised over $23 billion, but its bond spreads have blown out past 756bps, signaling deep market anxiety about repayment. 
* **Oracle** saw its credit rating downgraded to just one rung above junk by S&P Global, largely due to its massive data center buildout for OpenAI.

Compounding this debt crisis is a hardware-induced supply chain loop. The massive demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and DRAM by AI servers has triggered fierce price gouging by the Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung triopoly. 
* A single 72-GPU NVL72 server requires over 20 terabytes of HBM and 17 terabytes of LPDDR5X RAM.
* This creates a destructive feedback loop: **The more AI servers hyperscalers buy, the more expensive RAM becomes, which drives up server costs, forcing companies to borrow even more money.**

## The Final Boss of Circular Financing

The desperation to manufacture sustained demand has led to increasingly bizarre financial engineering. Reports surfaced that NVIDIA was in talks to guarantee **$250 billion in financing** for a 10GW data center built by SoftBank affiliate SB Energy—meaning NVIDIA would backstop the lease payments so SB Energy can raise debt to buy chips from NVIDIA to rent to OpenAI.

This is the ultimate evolution of circular financing:
1. **SoftBank** (which holds billions in OpenAI stock) uses its subsidiary to raise debt for a data center.
2. **NVIDIA** (which invests in OpenAI and supplies its chips) provides the financial backstop.
3. **OpenAI** (unprofitable and lacking an investment-grade rating) leases the facility.
4. **SoftBank** plans to take SB Energy public using the OpenAI contract to unlock equity gains.

When you strip away the corporate shell game, the math breaks down entirely. OpenAI cannot afford the tens of billions in annual compute costs required to sustain these gargantuan facilities without an endless stream of venture capital injections and corporate backstops.

## AI Is Running Out of Time to Actually Matter

Outside of a few corporate boardrooms and tech-Twitter echo chambers, generative AI has failed to deliver genuine, widespread productivity gains. 

As software engineer and consultant Nik Suresh noted after observing a 0% success rate across enterprise AI projects:
> *"In every sufficiently large business we have observed... continued advancement, and increasingly continued employment, has started to require repeated professions of belief in the transformative power of AI... declarations of faith."*

Businesses are falling prey to "AI-washing," where executives lie about productivity boosts out of fear of professional ostracization, while actual software projects buckle under the weight of AI's novelty, high costs, and systemic unreliability. 

Ratings agencies like **Fitch** have begun sounding the alarm, warning that the heavy reliance on AI-driven capex creates a profound vulnerability for corporate credit markets if a long-overdue market correction hits.

### The Inevitable Reckoning

If the music stopped tomorrow—if AI vanished from the news cycle and corporate boardrooms—would the underlying economy miss it? The core utility of LLMs remains a niche, expensive tool trying desperately to pose as a general-purpose technology.

The trillion-dollar AI trade is an exercise in *kayfabe*: a shared mass delusion maintained because admitting failure would shatter stock prices and force tech monopolies to confess that their hypergrowth era has drawn to a close. Eventually, the bills will come due, the debt markets will slam shut, and the architects of the AI bubble will have to face the music.
