**Date:** 25 July 2026

### Summary
In this edition of *Pluralistic*, Cory Doctorow explores the intersection of predatory lending and digital control. As Apple expands into the leasing market, new code in iOS suggests the company is implementing "robo-repo" features—allowing lenders to remotely restrict or brick devices if payments are missed. This evolution of "usury-tech" leverages the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) to prevent users from modifying their own hardware, effectively turning consumer devices into tools for financial exploitation.

---

## Apple's Robo-Repo
Lenders have long sought to maximize the "risk premium"—the extra interest charged to those with poor credit—while minimizing the actual risk of default. Historically, this has been achieved through collateralization, such as subprime mortgages or "contract buying" in the trucking industry.

Today, technology has streamlined this exploitation. By utilizing digital networks, lenders can now track, threaten, or immobilize assets remotely. From subprime auto-loans that use GPS and remote kill-switches to Tesla’s "Smart Summon" repo capabilities, the cost of repossession has plummeted, enabling even riskier lending practices.

Apple is now entering this space. By controlling the software layer of its devices, Apple is creating a "Partner Finance Lock" that allows third-party lenders to disable apps and services on iPhones and iPads. This is the logical conclusion of Apple’s long-standing war against device ownership; by preventing users from modifying their own hardware, they have created a platform where the manufacturer—not the owner—retains ultimate control.
