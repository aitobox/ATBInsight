# Pluralistic: Discernment (28 Jul 2026)

### Summary
In this edition of *Pluralistic*, Cory Doctorow explores the critical role of **discernment** when interacting with AI. He argues that while Large Language Models (LLMs) can be useful tools for experts—such as for spellchecking or organizing personal archives—they are inherently dangerous when used by those who lack the expertise to verify their output. Doctorow critiques the push to use AI as a "teacher" or "teaching assistant," noting that students, by definition, lack the subject-matter expertise required to distinguish between accurate information and "hallucinations." He concludes that AI is a normal technology: useful in the hands of a skilled practitioner, but a potential nightmare when it replaces the human expertise necessary to guide learning and creative work.

---

## Discernment
The core of the issue lies in the gap between "useful output" and "math-flavored word salad." Doctorow uses the example of a complex mathematical conjecture to illustrate that without deep, foundational knowledge, one cannot distinguish between a brilliant insight and a nonsensical error. 

He addresses common criticisms regarding his own use of local, open-source AI for mundane tasks like spellchecking, clarifying that he uses these tools as a supplement to—not a replacement for—his own expertise. He emphasizes that the "enshittification" of software like Microsoft Word, which forces unwanted AI suggestions onto users, highlights the danger of relying on automated systems that lack context.

**Key Takeaways:**
* **Expertise is the filter:** AI is only as reliable as the user's ability to verify its output.
* **The teacher paradox:** Using AI to teach those who are still learning is fundamentally flawed because the learner cannot yet exercise the necessary discernment.
* **The "Normal Technology" rule:** Like any other tool, AI has specific use cases where it excels and others where it is actively harmful.
