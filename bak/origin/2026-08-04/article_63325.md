# Can the Tide of AI Investment Lift All Boats on the Web?

## Summary
In an insightful discussion on the future of AI and the web, Jason Grigsby argues that AI agents should rely on existing web standards and assistive technologies rather than receiving bespoke APIs. By channeling heavy AI investments into general web platform enhancements, we can create solutions that benefit everyday users and developers alike—reinforcing the core philosophy that the web is for everyone.

---

## The Core Argument: AI as Assistive Technology

In a [recent article by Jason Grigsby](https://cloudfour.com/thinks/improvements-to-web-for-ai-should-benefit-all-users/), the Safari team's perspective on AI agents offers a guiding principle for the web platform:

> An agent acting on a user’s behalf is, in effect, **assistive technology**: it should operate a site as the user would, and the site should not single it out for different treatment.

Rather than creating custom solutions or "training wheels" exclusively for AI, the web standards community should focus on strengthening existing technologies—such as APIs and semantic markup—to close current platform gaps for everyone. 

## A Rising Tide for the Web Platform

The trillions of dollars currently being poured into artificial intelligence could be funneled toward improving the foundational web technologies that agents already rely upon. The ultimate goal is simple: let the rising tide of AI investment **lift all boats**. 

As Grigsby points out:

> If we’re solving this problem for AI, perhaps we can find a solution that works for end users too.

This aligns seamlessly with the web’s traditional priority of constituents, placing **User Experience (UX)** above Developer Experience (DX) and, by extension, Agent Experience (AX).

## The Irony of AI "Independence"

Grigsby also highlights the inherent contradiction in how we build for AI:

> …let’s set aside for the moment the irony that AI is supposed to replace all of our jobs and become a super intelligence and at the same time we also need to add special AI training wheels for it to use the web.

It is a peculiar paradox: praising AI for its impending independence and supreme intelligence, while simultaneously coddling it with custom accommodations to help it navigate the open web.
