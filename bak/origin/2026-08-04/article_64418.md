# Pluralistic: Why businesses lie about AI (01 Aug 2026)

## Summary

In this edition of *Pluralistic*, Cory Doctorow explores the pervasive "AI mania" overtaking corporate leadership, drawing heavily on insights from tech consultant Nikhil Suresh. Despite billions spent and relentless executive hype claiming that AI is "changing everything," the reality on the ground is stark: enterprise AI projects boast a near-zero success rate, and companies are merely engaging in compliance-driven lying to appease true-believer bosses and protect their jobs. Doctorow contrasts this capital-driven delusion with the nuanced ways individual, skilled workers ("centaurs") successfully use automation on their own terms, warning that corporate AI adoption acts like asbestos—slowly poisoning technological infrastructure with defective chatbots and grift.

## Why Businesses Lie About AI

Neoclassical economics assumes rationality. The corollary of, *"If you're so smart, why aren't you rich?"* is *"you're rich, so you must be very smart!"* Thus it is that many people assume that if powerful, well-compensated CEOs insist that "AI is changing everything," well then, *AI must be changing everything*.

But the evidence for this "changing everything" thesis is thin on the ground. Despite a global mania that has reduced the real, pressing need for digital sovereignty to the imaginary need to create "sovereign AI," no one can really articulate the case for it. If Donald Trump ordered Big Tech to turn off all of your country's chatbots tomorrow, nothing would change. 

Contrast this with what would transpire if Trump directed his tech giants to switch off your country's Office 365 access, or to brick your Android and iOS phones, or to killswitch your John Deere tractors. Your country would effectively cease to exist. Read more about this in [Their Trillions, Our Billions](https://pluralistic.net/2026/06/18/their-trillions-our-billions/#eyes-on-the-prize).

The world is full of people who insist that "AI is changing everything" but who—when pressed—have to admit that what they mean is that they're pretty sure that AI *will* change everything. Eventually. After we allow it to consume all the planet's energy, carbon, water, and financial resources.

### The Real Corporate AI Situation

Nikhil Suresh from Hermit Tech, a consulting firm of "radically ethical data wizards," has observed the shear between the stated business/AI situation and the *real* one. In his essay, ["AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decisionmaking"](https://hermit-tech.com/blog/ai-mania-is-eviscerating-global-decisionmaking), Suresh reports on conversations with hundreds of executives and subordinates. 

Suresh is well-known for his trenchant AI criticism, including:
*   ["I Will Fucking Piledrive You If You Mention AI Again"](https://ludic.mataroa.blog/blog/i-will-fucking-piledrive-you-if-you-mention-ai-again/) (2024)
*   ["Contra Ptacek's Terrible Article On AI"](https://ludic.mataroa.blog/blog/contra-ptaceks-terrible-article-on-ai/)

The core thesis of Suresh's latest piece is captured here:

> "[W]e’re facing a coordination problem around executives being honest around the AI gains they’ve witnessed – if they co-operate, they keep their jobs. If they defect, they will possibly be fired by their embarrassed peers (who have now been implicitly called liars, cowards, or incompetents) and then replaced with someone that will toe the line anyway. If they could all admit the truth at once there might be some hope, but there is no way to coordinate that event."

### Highlights of Corporate Delusion

*   **Religious Mania for Buzzwords:** Businesses pivot to "agentic workflows" despite market indifference because "every company has to do something agentic now." Employees who voice honest objections are passed over or laid off; promotions are reserved for those making religious declarations of faith in AI.
*   **A 0% Success Rate:** Suresh reports a *zero percent success rate* across enterprise AI projects over a year and a half. Companies are terminally bad at running software projects; adding AI simply introduces new failure points.
*   **"AI Washing" Workers:** Frustrated workers simply do their jobs normally and claim Claude did it, sometimes spinning up circular chatbot processes solely to consume tokens and score high on corporate token leaderboards.
*   **The Hypnotizing Demo:** Executives lukewarm on millions in real-world savings are completely mesmerized by unreliable AI demos (like Snowflake's Cortex), driven by a deep-seated fear that they are falling behind.

### Why Do Bosses Fall for It?

Doctorow posits two underlying reasons for this corporate psychosis:

1.  **Billionaire Solipsism:** Powerful leaders hate ego-shattering conflicts with human subordinates who know how things actually work. Conversational AI interfaces like Cortex *never* back-sass leadership about foolish requests. The fact that they misinform 8% of the time is a small price to pay for life without critical human feedback.
2.  **The Centaur Phenomenon:** Individual coders and specialists *do* report success with AI tools, but only because they act as "centaurs"—experienced workers maintaining strict **discernment** and using automation on their own terms. Worker-driven automation improves quality, while capital-driven automation blindly seeks throughput by cutting staff.

Ultimately, AI in the enterprise is the asbestos we are shoveling into the walls of our technological society. Future generations will spend decades digging it out.
