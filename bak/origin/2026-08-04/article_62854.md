# Pluralistic: AI Solipsists and AI Cynics (24 Jul 2026)

## Summary

In this essay, Cory Doctorow argues that while artificial intelligence as a standalone technology is fairly ordinary, the massive multi-trillion-dollar AI investment bubble is a uniquely destructive economic pathology. Doctorow explores the psychological and financial drivers behind this speculative frenzy, suggesting it is fueled by a mix of "billionaire solipsism"—where the ultra-wealthy fail to view ordinary people as fully real—and deep-seated market cynicism, where investors care less about whether AI actually works and more about whether they can convince gullible corporate bosses to buy into the scam. Ultimately, he warns of the devastating long-term consequences the AI bubble will leave behind once it inevitably bursts.

## AI Solipsists and AI Cynics

![A carny barker selling tickets, with his head replaced by HAL9000's red eye](http://localhost/proxy/7yG8IPPCOZpp5f_mmaN-92NpyAi1Y8aaWa2Yy9rhuzU=/aHR0cHM6Ly9pMC53cC5jb20vY3JhcGhvdW5kLmNvbS9pbWFnZXMvYWlfY2FwZXguanBnP3c9ODQwJnNzbD0x)

As a *technology*, AI isn't exceptional. It’s neither exceptionally wicked nor exceptionally good. Take away the galactic-scale stock swindle, and we would simply call AI applications "plug-ins," using and abusing them much like any other past technology. 

As a destructive economic pathology, however, AI is *extraordinary*. Boosters have poured over $1.4 trillion into the promise of automating away workers while slashing the wages of those who remain—despite the glaring reality that AI often cannot reliably perform the jobs it is meant to replace. Corporations and monopolies are betting they can worsen their products without regulatory consequence because they are the only game in town. 

To feed this bubble, regional governments have trampled environmental and planning laws to build endless data centers that may soon sit abandoned. Without the desperate imperative to turn trillions in losses into profits, we wouldn't be seeing the aggressive data-scraping epidemic, the forced cramming of chatbots into every consumer product, or the rash firing of productive human workers.

### Why Do Billionaires Love AI?

The single most salient fact about AI is the investment bubble itself, not its statistical capabilities. This raises a pressing question: why are the world’s wealthiest investors handing over trillions to finance it?

1. **Billionaire Solipsism:** Achieving extreme wealth often requires inflicting pain on vast populations. To look in the mirror without remorse, billionaires frequently fail to view ordinary people as fully real. Whether it's Leona Helmsley declaring "only the little people pay taxes" or Elon Musk labeling critics "NPCs," billionaire solipsism runs deep. Social media executives love AI precisely because it promises to replace unpredictable human friends with pliable chatbots engineered to maximize platform engagement and ad revenue.
2. **The Keynesian Beauty Contest (AI Cynicism):** Many investors don't actually believe in the technical promises of AI; instead, they believe that *other* people will believe in them. Following John Maynard Keynes' principle of the beauty contest, the goal isn't to pick the best business, but to pick the one *other investors* will pile into—allowing cynical speculators to cash out before the inevitable crash.

Bosses are absolute suckers for this scam because they hate negotiating with workers who know how things actually work and point out when executive plans are illegal, dangerous, or stupid. AI offers bosses the fantasy of a toy steering wheel wired directly into the corporate engine, letting them run the company entirely on their own.

### The Aftermath

AI is even worse than alternative-medicine scams like Gwyneth Paltrow's jade eggs or Andrew Tate's overpriced supplements. When the AI bubble bursts—vaporizing a third of the U.S. stock market—governments will reflexively turn to austerity, paving the way for fascism. Long after the chatbots vanish, civilization will be left dealing with catastrophic carbon emissions that permanently alter our ice caps, proving that while AI is an ordinary technology, the AI bubble is extraordinarily toxic.
