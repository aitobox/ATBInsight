# Pluralistic: The stupidest imaginable excuses for surveillance pricing (30 Jul 2026)

## Executive Summary
This edition of *Pluralistic* by Cory Doctorow explores the rise of **surveillance pricing**—the practice of using AI and vast data-broker dossiers to dynamically price goods and services based on consumer vulnerability and willingness to pay. Focusing on California’s anti-surveillance pricing bill (AB-2564) and San Francisco’s stalled support for it due to pushback from the local Chamber of Commerce, Doctorow unpacks how businesses use automated price gouging, debunking corporate excuses and emphasizing that privacy is a fundamental human right.

## The Stupidest Imaginable Excuses for Surveillance Pricing

With "surveillance pricing," businesses have finally found something AI can do way, way, *way* better than people: price gouging.

As the name suggests, "surveillance pricing" is the practice of charging every customer a different price for every transaction, based on the massive surveillance dossiers that Big Tech companies and data brokers have assembled on everyone in the world. Congress hasn't updated federal consumer privacy law since 1988, so pretty much any form of consumer surveillance is fair game.

### How AI Enables Multivariate Price Gouging
AI is indisputably great for multivariate statistical analysis. You can feed an AI behavioral data (where you go, what you do, what you buy, who you talk to) about all of your customers and ask it to cluster them by shared traits. Then, you can direct the AI to run continuous small experiments to discover the maximum markup each group will stomach.

Crucially, you don't even have to program bias into the AI; it will automatically find the most vulnerable people and rip them off the most. For example:
* Ecommerce sites have been caught charging parents of newborns extra when they order thermometers in the middle of the night.
* Instacart has marked up items for certain shoppers by as much as a third.

The goal is to shift all "consumer surplus" to corporations—charging you according to the desperation of your need.

### The Legislative Battle: California's AB-2564
While shoppers hate surveillance pricing, corporations are slavering for it. When Delta announced plans to surveillance-price airline seats, they faced such massive backlash that they quickly backtracked. 

While Maryland passed a weak, loophole-ridden bill, California is actually doing something. **AB-2564** is a smart, well-written bill that bans surveillance pricing while carving out legitimate reasons for price differences (such as delivery costs or standard early-bird discounts). 

### San Francisco and the Chamber of Commerce
To pass state-level bills, municipal support is vital. The San Francisco Board of Supervisors was on the verge of passing a resolution supporting AB-2564 until the San Francisco Chamber of Commerce stepped in with a letter raising spurious, misleading objections. 

Fortunately, the Electronic Frontier Foundation (**EFF**) dismantled the Chamber’s FUD (Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt) in a letter to the Supervisors. The EFF clarified that:
1. Surveillance pricing does not lower prices; it preys on vulnerable consumers.
2. Privacy is a human right, not a luxury reserved for those who can afford to pay extra to opt out.
3. The bill contains clear carve-outs that protect standard discounting practices (like senior discounts or loyalty programs).

Surveillance pricing is offensive because it treats some people's dollars as worth half as much as others. San Francisco’s government—and lawmakers everywhere—should stand up against AI-equipped price gougers and support protections like AB-2564.
