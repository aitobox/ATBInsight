# LLMs Reward Expertise

### Summary
While Large Language Models (LLMs) have democratized entry-level tasks—turning everyone into a generalist—there is a common misconception that "prompt engineering" is a skill independent of domain knowledge. In reality, the most effective way to leverage an LLM is through deep expertise. By understanding the nuances of a specific field, a user can steer the model, identify errors, and extract high-level insights that remain inaccessible to the uninitiated.

---

### The Myth of the "Generalist" Tool
In the 2010s, technical gaps required reliance on skilled colleagues or internet searches. Today, LLMs allow anyone to generate "good enough" code or writing. Because these models are accessible to everyone, many assume that "skilled prompting" is a universal technique. However, this ignores the reality that the quality of the output is fundamentally capped by the user's ability to evaluate and iterate upon it.

### The Terence Tao Benchmark
A compelling case study is [Terence Tao’s conversation with ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/share/6a5fdc7a-d6f8-83e8-bbea-8deb42cfed56) regarding the Jacobian Conjecture. His interaction demonstrates that expertise changes the model's behavior:

*   **Conciseness:** By signaling high-level mathematical fluency, Tao forces the model out of "explaining-to-amateurs" mode and into a rigorous, peer-to-peer dialogue.
*   **Strategic Steering:** Tao does not blindly follow the model; he pushes back on complex outputs and makes his own leaps, treating the model as a collaborator rather than an oracle.
*   **The "Weird" Detector:** The true skill lies in identifying when the model’s reasoning is flawed or inefficient—a task only possible if the user already understands the underlying domain.

### Domain Knowledge as a Force Multiplier
This principle applies beyond mathematics. Whether in software engineering or system design, having a [theory of your codebase](https://seangoedecke.com/programming-with-ai-agents-as-theory-building/) allows you to push an LLM significantly harder. 

System design is dominated by concrete specifics rather than generic principles. An expert can ask, "Does X work here?" or "Can we express this in these familiar terms?"—questions that require a deep, contextual understanding of the project. While an LLM can help a novice get *something* done, it allows an expert to wring out superior, tailored solutions.

### The Human Bottleneck
As models continue to improve, the human remains the primary bottleneck. The information required to solve a problem often exists within the model, but extracting the *correct* solution requires a sophisticated human to act as a curator and guide. 

Expertise is not being rendered obsolete; it is being repurposed. We are moving toward a future where the value of a professional is defined by their ability to steer, validate, and synthesize the output of powerful, general-purpose intelligence.
