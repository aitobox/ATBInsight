# Why Apple Requires a Big Three Carrier Account to Lease an iPhone

## Summary
When leasing an iPhone through Apple's upgrade programs, customers are currently required to have an active cellular account with one of the "Big Three" U.S. carriers (AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon). While credit-risk assessment and fraud prevention play significant roles in this policy, the primary driver is the multi-million-dollar co-marketing and partnership agreements negotiated annually between Apple and the major carriers. 

---

## 1. The Mystery of the Carrier Requirement
When examining the Apple Upgrade program, a lingering question often arises: *Why does Apple care which cellular network you use?* 

Because customers lease their devices through Apple and financing partners like Klarna—rather than directly through the carrier—the strict requirement for a postpaid account with AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon initially seems unnecessary. Is it merely a simplistic credit-risk evaluation that unfairly penalizes prepaid and MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator) users? 

As it turns out, the reality involves a mix of severe retail fraud and high-stakes corporate negotiations.

---

## 2. The Multi-Million Dollar "Tier 1" Partnership
According to a former member of Apple's carrier relations team, the restriction is rooted in the massive financial agreements struck between Apple and major carriers every year. 

> *"Every year, the Carrier Teams at Apple negotiate a deal/program (hundreds of millions of dollars) with each of those Big 3. In return for those dollars, Apple will make concessions exactly like this... It’s more like Apple is selling it as part of a package deal that’s exclusively offered to its biggest and best 'tier 1' partners."*

As part of these co-marketing contracts, Apple agrees to funnel financing customers toward the Big Three. This exclusivity also serves as a strategic "carrot" to motivate smaller regional carriers or MVNOs. 

However, the landscape may slowly be evolving. Some larger MVNOs are attempting to negotiate their way into Tier 1 status—though doing so likely requires substantial financial investment. 

### The Apple Card Exception
An interesting variation exists within Apple Card Monthly Installments (ACMI), which offers 0% APR financing. Alongside the Big Three, **Boost Mobile** is also included as an eligible carrier:

> *"In order to buy an iPhone with ACMI, you must select one of the following carriers (prepaid carrier plans are not supported): AT&T, Boost Mobile, T-Mobile, or Verizon. An iPhone purchased with ACMI is always unlocked..."*

---

## 3. Combating Retail Fraud
Fraud prevention is another critical piece of the puzzle. iPhones are prime targets for financial scams, and the introduction of in-store carrier financing historically caused a surge in fraudulent activity.

A former Apple Store retail employee shared insight into the scale of the issue prior to 2019:

> *"I’d estimate on some days a double digit percentage of in-person phone sales were fraudulent. This behavior absolutely exploded when you started to be able to do carrier financing in the store. They had stolen the identity of a legit account holder... They always wanted the highest end phone, and would pay the sales tax due at time of sale in cash. Never wanted any accessories. Never wanted to open the box or set up the phone."*

Without strict carrier activation and validation requirements, the scale of stolen devices—for which carriers ultimately absorbed millions of dollars in losses—would be far worse. Although fraud detection systems have advanced significantly in recent years, the carrier requirement remains a powerful structural deterrent.
