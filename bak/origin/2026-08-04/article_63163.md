## A History of Stagnant Revenue and Acquisitions

[Oracle’s revenue graph](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/ORCL/oracle/revenue?ref=wheresyoured.at) tells the true story:

* Following the [April 2009 acquisition of Sun Microsystems ($5.7B)](https://www.oracle.com/corporate/pressrelease/oracle-buys-sun-042009.html?ref=wheresyoured.at), Oracle’s revenues barely kept pace with inflation.
* The [December 2021 acquisition of Cerner ($28.3B)](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/oracle-buys-cerner-2021-12-20/?ref=wheresyoured.at) created [Oracle Health](https://www.oracle.com/health/?ref=wheresyoured.at), adding roughly $6 billion in annual revenue that operated at **40% lower margins** than Oracle’s legacy businesses. 
* Oracle immediately attempted to salvage those margins through [office closures](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/oracle-close-cerner-kansas-city-153020150.html?ref=wheresyoured.at) and [brutal layoffs](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/a-look-at-cerners-layoffs/?ref=wheresyoured.at).

## The AI Apocalypse Now

For years, Oracle’s strategy worked: buy flagging companies, absorb their revenue, squeeze customers, and lay off staff. They never overextended themselves, always kept a positive free cash flow, and avoided external hype cycles.

**Until AI.**

With generative AI, Oracle broke its cardinal rule. Instead of letting others innovate, it plunged headfirst into building cloud infrastructure. In its last fiscal year alone, Oracle’s capex hit **$55.6 billion**—more than it spent in the previous nine years combined ($50.4 billion). 

* **Tripling Debt:** Debt skyrocketed from $56.91 billion in FY2017 to **$167.4 billion** in FY2026.
* **Negative Cash Flow:** FY2026 ended with a free cash flow of **negative $23.69 billion**. For context, Oracle had maintained positive free cash flow every single year *since 2001, surviving both the Great Financial Crisis and COVID.*

### The OpenAI Trap
Oracle has committed to building 7.1 GW of data center capacity for a single client—OpenAI—as part of a [**$300 billion, five-year contract**](https://www.wsj.com/business/openai-oracle-sign-300-billion-computing-deal-among-biggest-in-history-ff27c8fe?ref=wheresyoured.at). It requires building an impossible amount of physical infrastructure in an impossible timeframe for a client that cannot sustainably afford the $70+ billion in annual costs required to justify it.

With plateauing legacy revenues, revolting customers, and a staggering $95 billion projected capex for FY2027, Oracle is gambling its entire existence on the AI bubble. 

This may very well be Larry Ellison’s final dance with Silicon Valley.
