# ‘Who’s Afraid of Chinese Models?’

## 📝 Summary
Ben Thompson of *Stratechery* highlights a striking paradox in the AI industry: Western open-weight model makers are hamstrung by frontier labs' terms of service and copyright restrictions, forcing them to rely on Chinese models (like Kimi K3) which freely distill U.S. technology. Rather than fighting an unwinnable war against distillation, the U.S. should reform its copyright laws to treat AI training as fair use and prohibit anti-distillation clauses, thereby fueling domestic innovation.

---

## 🔬 The Distillation Paradox
Writing on the hype surrounding Kimi K3, Ben Thompson points out a fundamental disadvantage faced by Western developers:

> "This is a point that bears repeating: because U.S. open weight model makers must follow the frontier labs’ terms of service, they (1) are worse than Chinese alternatives and (2) end up distilling the distillation, just with a detour through Chinese labs. Wouldn’t it be better if western open weight model makers could go to the source?"

Thompson questions the moral panic surrounding distillation, noting that Large Language Models are themselves simply distilled versions of the open internet. 

> "To that end, here’s an even more interesting question around distillation: why exactly is it bad? After all, what are large language models but the distillation of all of the knowledge on the open Internet, scraped by the frontier labs and distilled into the models that are themselves being distilled? Who is exactly being wronged here?"

---

## 🛠️ A Call for Policy Reform
To resolve this imbalance, Thompson proposes a bold shift in U.S. copyright and tech policy:

* **Protect Training as Fair Use:** Pass legislation explicitly establishing that collecting data to train models falls under fair use.
* **Ban Anti-Distillation Clauses:** Bar terms of service that forbid distillation (at a minimum for U.S. companies). 

Because stopping distillation—which is fundamentally just querying an API—is virtually impossible, the U.S. should embrace a new copyright policy. This approach would indemnify labs while ensuring that accumulated knowledge continues to drive broader innovation.

---

## 🇨🇳 Beyond Copying: The Chinese Strategy
It is worth noting—as Thompson does—that leading Chinese models like K3 are not merely cheap rip-offs; their success is not *solely* built on distillation. However, distillation remains a crucial mechanism allowing them to consistently release models that trail the U.S. frontier by just 6 to 9 months. 

Because Chinese entities largely ignore Western terms of service and copyright restrictions, Western developers who *do* respect these rules find themselves waiting on Chinese releases just to access efficiently distilled models. 

Ultimately, this dynamic makes it difficult to sympathize with frontier giants like OpenAI and Anthropic when they complain about their models being distilled in violation of their terms of service.
