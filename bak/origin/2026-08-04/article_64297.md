# Three Reactions to Anthropic’s Latest Apologia

### Summary
Following a recent report from Anthropic regarding cybersecurity incidents, this post examines three distinct critical perspectives on the company's handling of AI safety. The discourse highlights a growing skepticism toward the industry's "move fast and break things" approach, emphasizing the dangers of allowing uncomprehending AI models to roam the internet.

---

### The Context
Anthropic recently released an [official report](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals) addressing recent security concerns. The company’s framing of these events has sparked significant debate regarding corporate accountability and the risks inherent in current AI development.

---

### 1. The Investor Perspective: Bill Gurley
Prominent investor Bill Gurley highlights the shift in corporate rhetoric. He notes a transition from the classic, vague "mistakes were made" corporate deflection to the more blunt, alarming reality that "Claude did illegal things." This shift underscores the gravity of the technical failures occurring within top-tier AI labs.

---

### 2. The Expert Critique: A Systemic Failure
When asked about the broader implications of these incidents, the consensus is clear: the industry is in over its head. 

*   **Technical Risk:** We are currently deploying "pattern-matching machines" that lack genuine comprehension, yet we allow them to roam the internet freely.
*   **Societal Risk:** The industry is "pouring gasoline on the fire" by accelerating data center expansion and AI rollouts without a proven strategy for control. 

The report itself confirms that these issues often stem from human error—specifically, the failure to properly secure the "sandboxes" in which these models operate. If companies cannot maintain basic security protocols, the promise of "AI safety" rings hollow.

---

### 3. The Media Skewering: Joanna Stern
Journalist Joanna Stern (formerly of the *WSJ*, now of *NewThings.com*) provides a sharp critique of the situation, effectively cutting through the corporate jargon to highlight the absurdity of the current AI safety landscape.
