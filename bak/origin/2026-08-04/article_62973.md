# AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decision-Making

*Source: [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/07/25/ai-mania-nikhil-suresh)*

---

## 📌 Executive Summary
The corporate world is currently gripped by an unquestioning, religious fervor surrounding generative AI. A recent essay by Nikhil Suresh highlights how fear of breaking the illusion—and risking multi-million-dollar enterprise contracts—forces executives into a conspiracy of silence regarding exaggerated productivity claims. Meanwhile, the perceived "magic" of AI is disproportionately dazzling non-technical corporate leaders, leading to widespread delusion that will persist until the market bubble inevitably bursts.

---

## 🏢 The Corporate Conspiracy of Silence
In a revealing anecdote from Nikhil Suresh’s overseas trip, a technically competent Fortune 500 executive explained why exorbitant claims about AI (such as "100× productivity gains") go completely unchallenged within the industry:

> "Executives at their *customers* were saying absurd things about achieving 100× productivity, and this meant that if any executive at the *vendor* said that these gains were not plausible, it would undermine the credibility of the customer’s executive, be perceived as an attack (or heresy), and possibly result in an enterprise contract cancellation."

Questioning the AI narrative is treated as corporate heresy. Dissenters remain silent because pointing out technological realities is a fast track to losing enterprise contracts—and getting fired.

---

## 🪄 Why Generative AI Feels Like "Magic"
While traditional computing has steadily revolutionized the world through Moore's Law and networking effects, it has largely remained a communication tool rather than a creative one for the average person. 

Generative AI changes this dynamic entirely. Fulfilling Arthur C. Clarke’s third law—*"Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic"*—tools like ChatGPT and Claude allow non-technical individuals to create and discover things previously beyond their grasp. 

However, this has created a uniquely dangerous psychological phenomenon among confident, non-technical corporate managers:
* **The "Big Bang" Illusion:** For executives with limited technical literacy, generative AI feels like the birth of creation itself.
* **Impervious to Reason:** Because the technology genuinely feels magical to them, it is impossible to convince these leaders that current AI models—while impressive and useful—are several orders of magnitude less revolutionary than their imaginations suggest. 

Until the bubble bursts, the corporate world remains trapped in an AI mania that punishes pragmatism and rewards self-deception.
