# China Has All But Caught Up. The US Is Not Going to “Win” the AI War. Here’s What We Should Do Instead.

By Gary Marcus

---

## 📌 Executive Summary

* **The Catch-Up is Complete:** Recent breakthroughs by Chinese firms like Moonshot.AI (Kimi K3), Z.ai (GLM 5.2), and Alibaba (Qwen) demonstrate that China has essentially closed the gap with the best American AI models—often offering high-performing, open-weight models for free.
* **The Death of the Silicon Valley "Moat":** The belief that the US would secure a permanent, monopolistic victory in AI via Large Language Models (LLMs) has proven unfounded. Unprofitability, a lack of technical moats, and aggressive global competition are destabilizing the US GenAI industry.
* **Strategic Policy Options:** The US government faces difficult choices moving forward, ranging from doing nothing or erecting regulatory moats, to nationalizing labs or shifting toward a global public good model akin to "CERN for AI."

## The Illusion of US AI Supremacy

Three weeks ago, I warned about the inevitable fallout of an industry built on zero technical moats, hyper-competition, and scarce profits:

> *"The ultimate culmination of the 'no moat $\rightarrow$ more competitors $\rightarrow$ price wars $\rightarrow$ profits are scarce' argument that I have been making here regularly since the summer of 2023, has arrived—and may wreck the U.S. AI industry."*

Last week, Chinese company Moonshot.AI announced **Kimi K3**, a model roughly on par with top American alternatives. Uniquely, it is an **open-weight** model that consumers can download and run locally for free (provided they have the necessary hardware). This follows waves made by Z.ai's GLM 5.2 and Alibaba's Qwen. 

This rapidly shifting landscape undermines the business models of OpenAI and Anthropic, casts doubt on their upcoming IPOs, and shatters the notion that America can definitively "win" a zero-sum AI race. 

### Why This Shouldn't Be a Surprise

My January 2025 essay—written right after the DeepSeek release—laid out precisely what is happening today. At the time, I predicted that:
* The US would not achieve a decisive victory over China by hyper-focusing exclusively on LLMs.
* The two nations would converge on a technical tie.
* The lack of a technical moat would keep companies like OpenAI unprofitable.
* Nvidia would face vulnerabilities.
* The CHIPS Act would backfire without effectively holding China back.
* Models would grow cheaper and more efficient, but persistent issues like hallucinations and unreliability would remain.
* Endless repetition of LLM scaling would starve funding from more original ideas.

Unfortunately, a government heavily intertwined with Silicon Valley groupthink failed to heed these warnings. By going "all-in" on GenAI, the US economy has been placed in a precarious position while competitors abroad pull ahead.

## Questions Congress Should Be Asking

Before deciding on a path forward, lawmakers must investigate how the US squandered its initial lead:
* Were fundamental strategic or tactical errors made?
* Was banking everything on a single, easily replicable technology a mistake?
* Did US companies do enough to protect their intellectual property?
* To what degree are Chinese companies state-subsidized? To what extent was espionage involved?
* Have restrictions on immigration created a fatal talent drain? *(Note: Kimi’s founder studied at Carnegie Mellon before returning to China.)*
* What lessons can be applied to upcoming technological shifts, such as quantum computing or nuclear fusion?

## Seven Strategic Options for the US Government

As the administration assesses its next moves, seven distinct paths emerge—ranging from laissez-faire capitalism to radical global cooperation.

### 1. Do Nothing
Let OpenAI and Anthropic stand on their own feet without government bailouts or backdoor subsidies. If they find niches (like forward-deployed enterprise contracts) or fail, so be it. Amazon, Google, and Microsoft will remain standing to handle domestic and classified needs where Chinese models are excluded. *Drawback: Concedes a massive portion of the global AI market to China.*

### 2. Outlaw Open Source
Try to ban open-source AI. *Drawback:* Good luck with that. The cat is out of the bag; bans would merely push development to the dark web and overseas jurisdictions.

### 3. Build a "Regulatory Moat"
Use the coercive power of the bureaucratic state to clear the American market of cheaper foreign competitors under the guise of safety or security. Critics argue this amounts to "corporate communism"—crushing independent startups, stifling innovation, inflating consumer prices, and protecting a select few failing giants.

### 4. Bail Out the Big AI Labs
Pour public funds into rescuing struggling proprietary labs. **Hard pass.**

### 5. Ban Chinese Models Altogether
While rumored to be under consideration, blanket bans will likely isolate the US ecosystem, stifle domestic innovation, and drive up consumer costs.

### 6. Buy Out OpenAI and Anthropic for Pennies on the Dollar
Given their heavy cash-burn models and lack of proven profitability, the government could acquire these labs for a fraction of their peak valuations and convert them into national research laboratories. *Drawback:* Significant risk of government overreach and mass surveillance abuse.

### 7. Shift Toward AI as a Global Public Good (A "CERN for AI")
Give up the zero-sum mindset and pivot toward international collaboration. As I proposed in *The New York Times* back in 2017:

> *"I look with envy at my peers in high-energy physics, and in particular at CERN... They pursue ambitious, tightly defined projects... and share their results with the world, rather than restricting them to a single country or corporation... An international A.I. mission could genuinely change the world for the better."*

Interestingly, recent remarks by Xi Jinping opening an international AI conference suggest China might be open to dialogue on the matter. 

If the administration wants a true paradigm shift, a bold stroke would involve outlawing *closed-source* AI globally—requiring all manufacturers to share their weights, architectures, and training data with accredited researchers—and partnering with China to dedicate AI to global scientific and medical advancement. 

As investor and CEO Phil Libin noted: 
> *"The American system benefits when there’s free flow of capital, ideas, and people. We don’t need to 'win' a zero-sum game. We win by transforming games to non-zero-sum."*
