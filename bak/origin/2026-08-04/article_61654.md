# Reading List: July 18, 2026

### Summary
This week’s roundup explores the intersection of industrial policy, housing, and manufacturing. Key highlights include a major setback for the "California Forever" project, the ongoing struggles of the U.S. EV sector, and the operational failures behind a $500 million artillery shell facility. We also examine the role of build-to-rent housing in social mobility and the evolving trends in how retirees manage their property assets.

## Housekeeping

## Housing and Cities
*   **Equity-Based Leasing:** Reports suggest a growing trend of landlords requesting equity shares in lieu of traditional rent, a practice reminiscent of early startup-era deals.
*   **California Forever Setback:** The ambitious project to build a new city in Solano County has lost a key industrial tenant. Defense startup Saronic Technologies has opted to move its $3.2 billion "Port Alpha" automated shipyard to Texas instead of California.
*   **Build-to-Rent and Education:** A new study in *Real Estate Economics* suggests that the expansion of single-family rental units (SFRs) provides a significant benefit to economically disadvantaged children by granting them access to higher-performing school districts.
*   **Boomer Housing Trends:** Contrary to historical patterns, the *Wall Street Journal* reports that Baby Boomers are increasingly choosing to age in place rather than downsizing to smaller homes upon retirement.

## Manufacturing
*   **EV Industry Pivot:** The U.S. electric vehicle sector is facing significant headwinds. Major manufacturers are reporting staggering financial losses—including $26 billion at Stellantis and $19 billion at Ford—following a rapid and costly "U-turn" in their electrification strategies.
*   **Lucid Motors:** Lucid has officially denied rumors of a potential bankruptcy or take-private transaction, clarifying that they are working with restructuring advisors solely to improve operational execution.
*   **Artillery Production Delays:** A $500 million facility intended to produce 155mm artillery shells has failed to yield a single unit two years after coming online. The issue stems from an attempt to repurpose legacy equipment rather than investing in a modern, purpose-built production line.
