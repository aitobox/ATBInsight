# Book Review: *Dungeon Crawler Carl* by Matt Dinniman
**Rating:** ★★⯪☆☆ (2.5 / 5)

---

## 📌 Executive Summary

Matt Dinniman’s *Dungeon Crawler Carl* is the literary equivalent of smashing your childhood action figures together—a derivative yet undeniably fun mash-up of *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy*, *The Hunger Games*, and modern gacha RPGs. While it delivers solid entertainment, goofy tropes, and campy villains, the relentless recitation of loot-box mechanics and stat-heavy combat ultimately bogs down the narrative, making the prospect of a dozen sequels a tedious endeavor.

---

## 🎮 A Mash-Up of Familiar Tropes

To call this book derivative is an understatement, but derivative is practically the currency of the LitRPG genre. The premise sets the stage immediately: 

* The Earth is obliterated by a bureaucratic alien coalition (shouts out to the Vogon Constructor Fleet).
* Survivors are forced into a deadly, televised survival game akin to *The Hunger Games*.
* The whole spectacle is broadcast live, complete with a Twitch-style narrator calling the action.

Every author has their archetype for the ultimate hero. Andy Weir relies on science-savvy boys; John Scalzi relies on pop-culture trivia; Matt Dinniman’s protagonist succeeds because he has simply played enough loot-box, gacha RPGs to exploit the system. 

---

## 👍 The Good: Splattertastic Fun

For the most part, the book is genuinely entertaining. Dinniman leans heavily into the absurdity of the genre:
* **The Action:** The gore is delightfully "splattertastic," thankfully stopping just short of genuine torture porn.
* **The Villains:** The antagonists are appropriately campy and theatrical.
* **The Sidekick:** The wisecracking companion manages to stay on the right side of the annoying line—most of the time.

---

## 👎 The Bad: Death by Spreadsheet

Unfortunately, the prose suffers from a critical flaw: **stat fatigue**. 

There is only so much purple prose and mechanical minutiae a reader can take. Reading endless variations of: 
> *"Then I severed a +3 damage with poison debuff while he raised a +1 shield and used his armour of holding to neutralise the attack..."*

Opening endless digital loot boxes feels like a chore when you're actually playing a video game. Reading through 150 sequential instances of *"I got a bronze box, then a gold box, then an enchanted trinket..."* becomes profoundly exhausting. 

This issue was magnified when trying the audiobook for the sequel, where the relentless recitation of items—effectively reading inventory lists aloud—proved completely unbearable.

---

## 🏁 Final Verdict

Look, I’ve never written a novel, and I heavily doubt I could do any better. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* has clearly found a massive, enthusiastic fanbase, and it’s easy to see why. As a standalone, it's a mildly fun read. But regrettably, I am not the target audience for endless stat-block squishing, recycled plots, and cackling video-game enemies on a dozen-book marathon.
