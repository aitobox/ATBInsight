# When The Future Doesn’t Need Us

## Summary
Historically, human leverage over the powerful has been rooted in our material necessity: factory workers can strike, truck drivers can halt economies, and citizens can ultimately withdraw their consent by withholding their labor. This thesis explores how Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) threatens to dismantle this foundational balance. By replacing human labor in the material economy and empowering mass surveillance, AGI risks rendering humanity materially powerless, potentially reducing liberal democracy to a fragile historical anomaly.

---

## 1. The Lever of Humanity
Today, the world is made of people, which grants individuals a vital measure of leverage:
* **Factory workers** can sabotage machines to halt production.
* **Truck drivers** can strike to freeze the entire economy.
* **Police** can stand back and [let protests topple governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan).
* **Militaries** can [mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_mutiny).

This sets a hard limit on how much the powerful can exploit the powerless. No matter how wealthy or well-armed leaders are, they remain embedded in society and depend on others to survive and maintain their quality of life. By withholding their contributions, ordinary people essentially hold a veto over the political order.

**AI changes this equation.** If AI runs the factories, drives the trucks, automates white-collar work, and fights wars, humans become materially powerless—we lose our ultimate leverage. While much of the anxiety surrounding AI focuses on poverty and meaning, the far greater threat is **disempowerment**.

---

## 2. The Relational Economy and Mass Displacement
What if mass unemployment doesn't happen? For centuries, automation has eliminated jobs, yet advanced economies maintain full employment by constantly discovering higher-level work. 

As [Alex Imas notes](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/what-will-be-scarce), commodity production will be automated, pushing income and employment toward the **relational sector**:
> *"As commodity production gets automated, income and employment flow toward the sectors with high income elasticity... the relational sector, including the arts but also care, education, hospitality, therapy, personal services, craftsmanship, and community, where the human element is part of the value."*

Durable future jobs won’t involve monitoring AI or prompt engineering; they will be rooted in human-centric roles like nurses, therapists, teachers, chefs, and artists. Thus, we may see mass job *displacement*, but not mass unemployment. However, the argument remains: **we would still be disempowered.**

---

## 3. The Argument: Why Post-AI Societies Lose Leverage
If all podcasters, fitness instructors, or craft brewers go on strike, nothing happens. The relational economy places workers further away from material reality. Unlike an assembly-line worker, a remote executive or a relational worker holds no material power to disrupt the physical infrastructure of society.

* **The Peasant Paradox:** Medieval peasants had immense material leverage (they grew all food) but lacked literacy and coordination. Industrial societies combined literacy, wealth, and concentration to successfully exploit their material power. Post-AI societies will be educated and connected, but **entirely lacking in material power**, leaving them unable to physically withdraw the consent of the governed.
* **Resistance is Futile:** People cannot resist economically because they are economically unnecessary. They cannot coordinate resistance because AGI makes mass surveillance a thousandfold more effective, assigning superhuman intelligence analysts to every human. Finally, automated drones controlled by AGI would easily overpower any violent civil uprisings.

Ultimately, power will rest exclusively with the few humans who issue commands to the AGI controlling the machinery of state and violence—and likely only until those humans are eventually usurped by their own AI systems. Liberal democracy may simply fade as a [spandrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spandrel_(biology)), sustained merely by institutional inertia rather than functional necessity.

## 4. Alternative Scenarios
If we develop powerful AGI and this dystopian disempowerment does not happen, why might that be? (Ordered by decreasing likelihood):

1. **Nothing ever happens:** Institutional inertia and robustness keep the system functioning. Boomers and non-working populations remain powerful because those with jobs and guns prefer stability.
2. **Enduring complementarity:** The "G" in AGI [is a myth](https://x.com/xuanalogue/status/2072101307695919278)—every mind has a different [shape](https://helentoner.substack.com/p/taking-jaggedness-seriously). AI handles bench-optimized tasks while humans retain abstract, high sample-efficiency roles.
3. **Classic doom:** We achieve powerful ASI and humanity goes extinct.
4. **The sysop scenario:** An aligned ASI takes over the world, establishing an inescapable utilitarian dystopia where we survive as pets (similar to [*The Metamorphosis of Prime Intellect*](https://localroger.com/prime-intellect/)).
5. **Deep learning hits a wall:** Progress stalls out due to fundamental technological bottlenecks.

## 5. The Argument Refined: Defining Power
To ground this rigorously, what does "empowerment" actually mean? 

Historically, political power requires violence, [*asabiyyah* (social cohesion)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asabiyyah), and coordination:
$$\text{Power} \iff \text{Violence} \land \text{Coordination}$$

In modern democracies, voters lack paramilitary violence, yet they retain a political voice. They achieve this through coordination (mass literacy, modern communication) combined with **work**—the reality that if people stop working, the state collapses. Thus, we can refine the formula:
$$\text{Power} \iff \left( \text{Violence} \lor \text{Work} \right) \land \text{Coordination}$$

For a group to be empowered, they need:
1. **Material leverage** (violent or economic) against the state.
2. **The ability to coordinate** to exploit that leverage.

While modern economies rely heavily on services (e.g., France's economy is ~80% services), the vital material base (truck drivers, dock workers) can still paralyze the country, allowing knowledge workers to effectively free-ride on that leverage. 

However, this suggests an unsettling conclusion: liberal democracies may remain stable *until* AGI completely replaces humans in material production. At that exact threshold, the state's structural dependency vanishes entirely, paving the way for unambiguous, permanent authoritarianism.
